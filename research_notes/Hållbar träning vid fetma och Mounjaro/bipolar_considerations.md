# Bipolar disorder considerations for an exercise and weight-loss app (Mounjaro user, 51 y, 140 kg)

Research date: 2026-11-12. Method: web search (about 20 queries). Full-text fetches failed for pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov and msjonline.org because those hosts are blocked in this session, so many findings rest on abstracts, registry pages and reputable secondary summaries. Each finding is graded:
- **[High]**: RCT, meta-analysis or regulator conclusion
- **[Moderate]**: large observational cohort or guideline
- **[Low]**: case reports, qualitative studies, pharmacovigilance or expert commentary
- **[Very low]**: single case or non-peer-reviewed source

The last section, **"App design implications"**, sits at the end of the final key question as its Inferences subsection, to keep the required three-part structure.

## 1. Exercise in bipolar disorder: depression benefit, hypomania risk, intensity, routine, sleep and timing

### Takeaway
Exercise is associated with fewer depressive symptoms and better functioning and quality of life in bipolar disorder. The evidence is mostly cross-sectional or cohort data plus small, heterogeneous trials, so it is low to moderate quality. People with bipolar disorder describe exercise as a "double-edged sword": it helps low mood but can feed escalating energy. There is also some evidence linking vigorous exercise with mania. The safest approach is moderate, regular and routine-anchored activity, with intensity reduced when mood or energy runs high.

### Cited Findings
- **Melo et al. 2016 systematic review** (J Affect Disord 198:32–38) **[Low–moderate]**:
  - It covered 31 studies with 15,587 patients. Sedentary lifestyle ranged from 40% to 64.9%.
  - It found only 3 prospective cohorts and no RCTs, and no intervention study tested exercise alone.
  - Exercise was associated with fewer depressive symptoms and better functioning and quality of life.
  - The evidence was "insufficient to establish a cause-effect relationship."
  - A summary notes "some evidence indicates a relationship between vigorous exercises and mania."
  - Sources: [UFC repository](https://repositorio.ufc.br/handle/riufc/20172?locale=en); [oasisbr record](https://oasisbr.ibict.br/vufind/Record/UFC-7_5f1b0f16314a49a44468efc28e34cb62)
- **Thomson et al. 2015 narrative review** (Front Psychol) **[Low]**:
  - At that time, "no existing randomized controlled trials have tested the impact of exercise on depressive, manic or hypomanic symptomatology."
  - Recommendations to exercise were "extrapolation from the unipolar literature, theory and clinical expertise."
  - [Frontiers 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00147/full); [PMC4349127](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4349127/)
- **Wright et al. 2009 systematic review** (cited within the above): one cohort found a higher risk of BD onset at 4-year follow-up among people who reported exercise at baseline. This is observational and confounded, and must not be read as exercise causing BD **[Very low]**. — [PMC4349127](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4349127/)
- **Vancampfort et al. 2016 meta-analysis** (J Affect Disord 201) **[Moderate]**: total daily physical activity did not differ between people with BD and controls, but adults with BD spend a lot of their waking hours sedentary. — [Manchester repository](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/publications/physical-activity-and-sedentary-behavior-in-people-with-bipolar-d/)
- **Fan & Deng 2025/26 meta-analysis** of exercise interventions on emotional symptoms in BD **[Moderate, limited by small trials]**:
  - Method: PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO-registered, RoB 2.0.
  - The authors call for "larger sample sizes and longer follow-up."
  - I could not read its manic-symptom outcomes (full text blocked).
  - [PMC12888045](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12888045/)
- **Wright, Armstrong & Taylor 2012**, "It's a double edged sword" (J Affect Disord 136(3):634–642) **[Low; qualitative, n≈25]**:
  - Three themes: "regulating exercise for mood regulation", "exercise as a double-edged sword", and "bringing structure to chaos."
  - Participants used exercise to lift low mood, but recognised it could amplify elevated states.
  - [PubMed 22100131](https://www.pubmed.gov/22100131/); [Plymouth portal](https://researchportal.plymouth.ac.uk/en/publications/its-a-double-edged-sword-a-qualitative-analysis-of-the-experience-2/)
- **Later University of Worcester interview work** **[Low]**: participants saw physical activity as helpful for depression and unhelpful for mania. — [Worcester blog](https://www.worc.ac.uk/about/news/academic-blog/health-and-wellbeing-blogs/Physical-activity-and-mental-health-exploring-bipolar-disorder.aspx)
- **Cochrane qualitative evidence synthesis** (McCartan et al. 2024, CD013557.pub2) on what influences physical-activity participation in BD **[Low–moderate confidence per GRADE-CERQual]**:
  - Barriers include physical-health limits, comorbidities and bipolar medication.
  - Keeping activity within one's regular routine supported keeping it up.
  - [Cochrane abstract PDF](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD013557.pub2/pdf/CDSR/CD013557/CD013557_abstract.pdf); [Unbound summary](https://evidence.unboundmedicine.com/evidence/view/Cochrane/435005/all/Factors_that_influence_participation_in_physical_activity_for_people_with_bipolar_disorder:_a_synthesis_of_qualitative_evidence)
- **Clinic blog (not peer reviewed)** **[Very low]**: claims intense exercise can worsen mania or hypomania, especially with poor sleep or excessive training. This is plausible but unverified. — [Ubie](https://ubiehealth.com/doctors-note/exercises-mood-regulation-bipolar-fails-medical-3732e3)
- **IPSRT (interpersonal and social rhythm therapy)** **[Moderate–high for IPSRT as a whole]**:
  - It stabilises daily rhythms such as wake time, first contact, meals, activity and bedtime, tracked with the Social Rhythm Metric.
  - Clinicians give guidance on sleep hygiene, nutrition and exercise as part of the daily routine.
  - In the 2007 STEP-BD trial (n≈175), patients who received intensive psychotherapy including IPSRT stayed well longer than those with clinical management alone.
  - [Carlat Report](https://www.thecarlatreport.com/articles/4901-treating-bipolar-disorder-with-interpersonal-and-social-rhythm-therapy); [IBPF](https://ibpf.org/ask-the-expert-what-is-ipsrt-therapy-and-how-can-it-benefit-those-with-bipolar/); [News-Medical 2007](https://www.news-medical.net/news/2007/12/10/33322.aspx)
- **NICE CG185 patient information**: people with bipolar disorder "should be offered advice on healthy eating and how to make sure you get enough exercise," and more support if they gain weight quickly. — [NICE IFP](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg185/ifp/chapter/taking-medication)

### Inferences
- The user's pattern of going all-in for 1–2 weeks and then quitting resembles the "double-edged sword" dynamic. A sudden burst of enthusiasm and high-intensity activity may be an early sign of mood elevation, not just motivation.
- The app should therefore:
  - cap progression, for example a maximum weekly increase in minutes;
  - reward consistency over intensity;
  - treat unusually high self-reported energy or reduced sleep as a cue to keep activity moderate and keep bedtime fixed, not as a cue to do more.
- For a 140 kg, mostly sedentary 51-year-old, low-impact moderate activity has the best risk-to-benefit balance from both a musculoskeletal and a mood standpoint. Examples: walking, chair or water exercise, light strength work. Vigorous training is not needed for the mood or metabolic benefit.
- Anchoring activity to a fixed daily time, ideally morning or daytime and in daylight, fits IPSRT principles: a regular rhythm, and a buffer before bedtime.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve Sylvia et al.'s NEW (Nutrition, Exercise and Wellness) treatment trials or Sylvia's exercise-in-BD reviews. They need direct PubMed lookup.
- No RCT directly measures whether exercise triggers hypomania. The evidence is qualitative and observational only.
- I found no BD-specific evidence on exercise timing and sleep. General-population meta-analyses on evening exercise were not verified in this session, so any "avoid vigorous exercise close to bedtime" rule is a precautionary inference, not BD evidence.
- I could not read the manic-outcome data in the Fan & Deng meta-analysis or the full Cochrane qualitative findings.

## 2. Lithium + GLP-1 RA / tirzepatide: toxicity risk, absorption and other medication interactions

### Takeaway
Since 2024 there has been a growing cluster of case reports and series of lithium toxicity after starting semaglutide or tirzepatide, or switching between them. These include at least four tirzepatide cases and one case needing haemodialysis. The proposed mechanism is dehydration from reduced eating and drinking, nausea, vomiting and diarrhoea, although some cases had no GI symptoms. Evidence is low (case reports), but the consequence is serious. The consensus advice is closer lithium-level monitoring around starting the GLP-1, each dose step and any switch, plus good hydration and early contact with the prescriber if GI symptoms appear. Tirzepatide also slows gastric emptying, and its label recommends monitoring narrow-therapeutic-index oral drugs at initiation and dose increases.

### Cited Findings
- **Davis et al.** (Colorado) **[Very low; case]**:
  - A woman aged 50 developed lithium toxicity about 3 weeks after starting tirzepatide.
  - Her lithium level rose with no change in lithium dose or kidney function.
  - It was described as the fourth reported tirzepatide-associated case.
  - [Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/glp-1s/glp-1-drugs-may-interact-with-a-common-psychiatric-medication/); [Rehab.com summary](https://www.rehab.com/weight-loss-drug-may-raise-lithium-risk-for-psychiatric-patients)
  - The patient profile closely matches this app's user.
- **Patel et al. 2026** (Int J Res Med Sci), "Lithium toxicity following initiation of tirzepatide in a patient with bipolar I disorder" **[Very low]**:
  - Toxicity occurred without the typical GI symptoms.
  - The authors note there are no published pharmacokinetic studies of the interaction.
  - They advise a "high index of suspicion" and proactive lithium monitoring.
  - [MSJ Online](https://www.msjonline.org/index.php/ijrms/article/view/17362) (abstract via search; full text not accessible)
- **Patarroyo-Rodriguez et al. 2026** (J Clin Psychopharmacol), "Tirzepatide-associated lithium toxicity necessitating hemodialysis in previously stable bipolar I disorder" **[Very low]**. — cited via [Psychopharmacology Institute](https://psychopharmacologyinstitute.com/section/can-glp-1-agonists-elevate-lithium-levels/) / search summary
- **Burson & Leung 2026** **[Very low]**: lithium toxicity after switching from semaglutide to tirzepatide in schizoaffective disorder, with the message that "switching between GLP-1 agents is not pharmacokinetically neutral." — [rethinkPeptides summary](https://rethinkpeptides.com/research/burson-2026-lithium-toxicity-following-a)
- **Semaglutide case series** (J Clin Psychopharmacol) **[Low]**: 3 patients had raised lithium levels after semaglutide was added, 2 with toxicity. One patient roughly halved their fluid intake after starting semaglutide. — [Psychopharmacology Institute](https://psychopharmacologyinstitute.com/section/can-glp-1-agonists-elevate-lithium-levels/); [apotheke adhoc (DE)](https://www.apotheke-adhoc.de/nachrichten/detail/pharmazie/semaglutid-achtung-wechselwirkung-mit-lithium/)
- **Mechanism and monitoring commentary** **[Low; expert opinion]**:
  - Mechanism:
    - Proposed: dehydration from appetite suppression, nausea (about 20% on semaglutide) and vomiting (about 6%).
    - Contradiction: GLP-1 RAs increase natriuresis, which would theoretically lower lithium, so the mechanism is "inconsistent."
  - Monitoring:
    - Recommendation: "increase monitoring rather than pre-emptively reducing dose."
    - Caution: routine checks every 6–12 months may miss changes in salt or water balance.
  - [Psychopharmacology Institute](https://psychopharmacologyinstitute.com/?p=1712567)
- **Suggested monitoring schedule** **[Low]**: measure a lithium level before starting the GLP-1 RA and recheck about 5 days after. — [JCP / Psychiatrist.com webinar](https://www.psychiatrist.com/insights/glp-1-receptor-agonists-for-long-term-kidney-protection/)
- **Drugs.com** rates lithium + semaglutide a "moderate" interaction. — [Drugs.com](https://www.drugs.com/drug-interactions/lithium-with-wegovy-1477-0-3878-19763.html)
- **NICE CG185 patient information on lithium** **[Guideline]**:
  - Drink enough water, "particularly after exercise, in hot weather or during illness."
  - Contact the doctor with diarrhoea or vomiting or if very unwell.
  - Lithium levels: weekly until stable, then every 3 months in year 1, then every 6 months. Every 3 months applies if aged 65 or over or with risk factors.
  - Check weight, urine and thyroid at least every 6 months.
  - [NICE IFP](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg185/ifp/chapter/taking-medication)
- **Tirzepatide EU SmPC and UK SmPC** **[Regulatory label]**:
  - Gastric emptying:
    - Tirzepatide delays gastric emptying and may affect absorption of oral drugs.
    - No dose adjustment is expected for most oral medicines.
  - Narrow-therapeutic-index drugs:
    - Monitor oral narrow-therapeutic-index drugs (e.g. warfarin, digoxin) "especially at initiation of tirzepatide treatment and following dose increase."
    - The UK SmPC advises more frequent INR monitoring for warfarin.
  - Oral contraceptives:
    - A single 5 mg dose reduced norgestimate exposure.
    - The EU label says this is not clinically relevant.
    - US Lilly guidance: switch to a non-oral method or add a barrier method for 4 weeks after starting and after each dose escalation.
  - Sources: [EU SmPC (EC register)](https://ec.europa.eu/health/documents/community-register/2022/20220915156773/anx_156773_en.pdf); [UK SmPC (MHRA)](https://mhraproducts4853.blob.core.windows.net/docs/71ef99936528ff04746645d0778bb798f81057d8); [Lilly Medical (US) on OCs](https://medical.lilly.com/us/products/answers/can-mounjaro-tirzepatide-be-used-with-oral-contraceptives-or-hormone-replacement-therapy-200617)
  - Contraception is probably less relevant at 51 but may matter for HRT.

### Inferences
- Lithium is not formally classed as a narrow-therapeutic-index drug in the tirzepatide label text I retrieved, but clinically it has a narrow therapeutic index. The label's principle of monitoring at initiation and dose steps should be applied, together with the dehydration risk.
- Risk windows for the app to flag:
  - the first weeks after starting Mounjaro;
  - each dose escalation (2.5 → 5 → 7.5 mg …);
  - any switch of GLP-1 product;
  - days with vomiting or diarrhoea, or very low food and fluid intake;
  - hot weather;
  - unusually heavy sweating from exercise.
- Rapid weight loss and much lower food intake, especially lower salt intake, may plausibly raise lithium levels. Classic lithium pharmacology holds that sodium depletion increases lithium reabsorption, but this was not verified in this session, so it is an inference.
- Signs of lithium toxicity overlap with GLP-1 side effects: nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, tremor, unsteadiness, confusion and drowsiness. The app should never let the user assume "it's just the Mounjaro."
- Other drugs:
  - Quetiapine and other antipsychotics have no known PK interaction with tirzepatide.
  - Constipation may add to antipsychotic-related constipation (inference).
  - With valproate or lamotrigine, any vomiting episode means missed or poorly absorbed doses and a risk of relapse or seizure. This applies especially to lamotrigine re-titration if doses are missed for several days, an inference from general lamotrigine practice that was not verified here.

### Gaps
- I found no Swedish-specific guidance on lithium + GLP-1 RA from FASS, Janusmed or Läkemedelsverket. The search did not surface Janusmed's entry, so a direct check at janusmed.se is needed.
- I found no MHRA Drug Safety Update specifically on lithium + GLP-1 RA.
- There are no pharmacokinetic studies of lithium with GLP-1 RAs, per Patel et al.
- I could not verify the exact count of published tirzepatide-lithium cases as of November 2026. Sources say "fourth reported."

## 3. GLP-1 RA psychiatric safety: suicidality reviews, serious mental illness, antipsychotic-induced weight gain

### Takeaway
Regulators found no causal link between GLP-1 RAs and suicidal thoughts or self-harm:
- EMA PRAC concluded so in April 2024 and made no label change.
- FDA reported no evidence in January 2024 but could not rule out a small risk.

Pooled RCT data (STEP trials) and large cohorts are reassuring. However, trials excluded people with serious mental illness. Data in bipolar disorder are emerging and so far favourable: a 2026 Swedish within-individual cohort found semaglutide associated with fewer psychiatric hospitalisations. GLP-1 RAs, especially semaglutide, are among the most effective treatments for antipsychotic-induced weight gain in RCTs and meta-analyses. Tirzepatide-specific data in bipolar disorder are absent.

### Cited Findings
- **EMA PRAC (April 2024)** **[High; regulator]**:
  - "The available evidence does not support a causal association" between GLP-1 RAs and suicidal or self-injurious thoughts or actions. No product-information update was needed.
  - The review started in July 2023 after reports from Iceland. It was inconclusive in December 2023.
  - The conclusion rested on two EHR studies, trials and post-marketing data.
  - [EMA PRAC highlights April 2024](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/news/meeting-highlights-pharmacovigilance-risk-assessment-committee-prac-8-11-april-2024); [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/ema-finds-no-link-between-suicidal-thoughts-self-harm-and-glp-1-drugs)
  - The listed molecules were dulaglutide, exenatide, liraglutide, lixisenatide and semaglutide. Tirzepatide was not named in the snippet retrieved.
- **FDA (January 2024)** **[Regulator]**: found no evidence that GLP-1 RAs cause suicidal thoughts or actions, but "cannot definitively rule out that a small risk may exist" and continued evaluating. — [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/ema-finds-no-link-between-suicidal-thoughts-self-harm-and-glp-1-drugs) (secondary; I did not retrieve the FDA document itself)
- **Wadden et al., JAMA Intern Med, September 2024** **[High for the trial population]**:
  - A post-hoc analysis of STEP 1–3 (n=3,377) and STEP 5 (n=304).
  - Suicidal ideation or behaviour occurred in 1% or fewer, with no difference from placebo.
  - Semaglutide-treated patients were less likely to move to a worse PHQ-9 category.
  - The trials excluded people with lifetime suicide attempts, recent MDD and "other clinically significant psychopathology." The authors call for study in SMI.
  - The analysis was funded by Novo Nordisk.
  - [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20240904/Semaglutide-does-not-increase-risk-of-depression-or-suicidal-thoughts.aspx); [Penn Medicine](https://www.pennmedicine.org/news/semaglutide-and-mental-health)
- **Pharmacovigilance and observational data** **[Low; signal detection]**:
  - A disproportionality analysis found 45% higher reporting odds for semaglutide-associated suicidal ideation. — [Penn Medicine / News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20240904/Semaglutide-does-not-increase-risk-of-depression-or-suicidal-thoughts.aspx)
  - A 2025 FAERS analysis (J Affect Disord) found signals only for semaglutide, not tirzepatide.
  - A 2026 FAERS analysis found about 0.3% suicidality-related reports among anti-obesity drug reports and judged the profile "neutral."
  - Source for both FAERS analyses: [PubMed 41739406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41739406/); [PubMed 40523410](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40523410/)
- **Tirzepatide** **[Moderate]**:
  - RCT pooled data: suicidal ideation in 0.6% (mostly low risk). Treatment-emergent psychiatric disorders did not differ from placebo. — [Healio, January 2026](https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20260130/tirzepatide-not-linked-to-increased-risk-for-depression-suicidal-ideation-in-obesity)
  - Real-world TriNetX cohort (Yu et al., Front Psychiatry 2025): possibly protective association, but observational. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1626103/full); [PMC12667738](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12667738/)
- **Bipolar-specific Swedish national cohort** (Taipale, Taylor et al., Acta Psychiatr Scand 2026; registers 2009–2024; within-individual design) **[Moderate]**:
  - Semaglutide was associated with a lower risk of psychiatric hospitalisation (aHR 0.79, 95% CI 0.69–0.91).
  - It was also associated with a lower risk of bipolar relapse (aHR 0.83, 0.69–0.99).
  - Liraglutide and dulaglutide showed no significant association.
  - Authors call for an RCT.
  - Tirzepatide was not reported.
  - [Healio, September 2026](https://www.healio.com/news/psychiatry/20260914/semaglutide-linked-to-positive-mental-health-outcomes-in-patients-with-bipolar-disorder); [PubMed 41921881](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41921881/); [CiteDrive DOI 10.1111/acps.70120](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/use-of-glucagonlike-peptide-1-receptor-agonists-and-the-associated-risk-of-hospitalisation-in-bipolar-disorder-from-a-nationwide-cohort-20092024/)
- **Antipsychotic-induced weight gain, network meta-analysis** (Stogios, Agarwal et al., JAMA Psychiatry 2026; 95 RCTs, about 5,900 patients, 39 interventions) **[Moderate–high; indirect comparisons]**:
  - Semaglutide gave the largest weight reduction vs placebo, more than 11 kg (about 24 lb).
  - The top five were metformin, topiramate, semaglutide, liraglutide and exenatide.
  - Only 3 semaglutide trials vs 16 for metformin.
  - [JAMA Psychiatry](https://media.jamanetwork.com/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2026.1814); [Managed Healthcare Executive](https://www.managedhealthcareexecutive.com/view/five-drugs-stand-out-for-antipsychotic-induced-weight-gain-study-finds)
- **GLP-1 RA meta-analysis in antipsychotic-treated patients** (Sampaio Sobral et al., Schizophr Res; 8 RCTs, n=523) **[Moderate]**:
  - Weight loss of −6.47 kg vs control.
  - Dual agonists such as tirzepatide had not been studied in this population.
  - A semaglutide-only meta-analysis (3 RCTs) reported −11.32 kg over 26–36 weeks, with more GI side effects.
  - [Psychopharmacology Institute](https://psychopharmacologyinstitute.com/section/schizophrenia-does-semaglutide-outperform-liraglutide-for-antipsychotic-weight-gain/); [Epocrates](https://www.epocrates.com/online/article/semaglutide-helps-best-for-antipsychotic-weight-gain)

### Inferences
- The best current evidence does not suggest GLP-1 RAs destabilise mood, and in BD it may suggest benefit. However, the user belongs to a group excluded from the pivotal trials. A light-touch mood check, rather than alarm, is proportionate, especially in the first months and after dose changes.
- Reduced appetite, nausea and fatigue from tirzepatide can mimic or mask depressive symptoms such as low appetite and low energy. The app's energy diary should let her tag "side-effect day" separately from "low-mood day" so that patterns are not misread, by her, by her son or by clinicians.
- Weight-loss expectations for people on antipsychotics may be somewhat lower than in STEP or SURMOUNT populations (−6 to −11 kg in GLP-1 RA trials in antipsychotic users vs about 15–20% body weight in general obesity trials). This is an indirect comparison.

### Gaps
- No tirzepatide data specific to bipolar disorder or SMI were found.
- I did not retrieve the FDA's January 2024 communication itself (secondary sources only).
- I did not confirm whether PRAC's 2024 conclusion explicitly covered tirzepatide.
- I did not find any 2024–2026 RCT of GLP-1 RA specifically in bipolar disorder. Ongoing trials were not checked.

## 4. Weight gain from mood stabilisers and antipsychotics: implications for expectations

### Takeaway
Many bipolar medications promote weight gain, with olanzapine and quetiapine at the top, valproate high, and lithium moderate or debated. Lamotrigine is roughly weight-neutral. Part of the user's weight likely reflects medication, and medication-driven appetite and metabolic effects may partly blunt the expected Mounjaro response. Expectations should be set accordingly, and the user should never stop or change psychiatric medication to lose weight.

### Cited Findings
- **Olanzapine**: about 4.2–7.4 kg in total, sometimes up to 12 kg. **Quetiapine**: about 4.1–5.6 kg. **Lamotrigine**: from −2 kg to +0.6 kg. **[Moderate; review]** — [PMC6493485](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6493485); [table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6493485/table/t1)
- **Carlat summary** **[Moderate; review]**:
  - Lithium: about 4.5–12 kg over long-term treatment in about 25% of patients (Torrent review).
  - Lamotrigine: generally no gain in large studies.
  - Quetiapine: 2.6 kg in 12 weeks.
  - [Carlat Report](https://www.thecarlatreport.com/articles/1533-weight-gain-and-mood-stabilizers)
- **Valproate**: 8–14 kg average in a subset of patients, with incidence 8–59%. — [Psychiatric Services 2002](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ps.53.7.842)
- **Valproate vs lithium** **[Moderate]**: valproate causes about 1.1 kg more than lithium. — [PMC3375952](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3375952)
- **Recent pharmacovigilance** **[Low]**: lithium had fewer severe weight-gain reports than olanzapine, quetiapine or valproate. This contradicts older beliefs. — [PMC10577117](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10577117)
- **NICE monitoring** **[Guideline]**:
  - For antipsychotics, weigh weekly for the first 6 weeks.
  - Check weight at least every 6 months.
  - Offer more support for rapid weight gain.
  - [NICE IFP](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg185/ifp/chapter/taking-medication); [NICE QS95 statement 7](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/qs95/chapter/quality-statement-7-assessing-physical-health)

### Inferences
- The app should frame medication-related weight gain as a side effect, not a personal failing. This is a core anti-shame message.
- Expectation-setting copy: "Some mood medicines make weight loss slower. Progress is still progress." Avoid showing population-average weight-loss curves from SURMOUNT trials as targets.
- Any thought of skipping mood medication to lose weight should trigger a gentle "talk to your doctor first" message. Non-adherence is a major relapse risk (general clinical knowledge, not sourced here).
- Medication changes by her psychiatrist may cause weight plateaus or rebounds that the app should normalise.

### Gaps
- There is no single head-to-head source with consistent kg figures across all drugs.
- I found no data on how much medication-induced weight gain attenuates tirzepatide response specifically.

## 5. Mood/energy self-monitoring in BD: digital tracking evidence, risks, scales, early warning, family access ethics

### Takeaway
RCTs of smartphone self-monitoring in BD (MONARCA I and II, RADMIS) show no clear benefit on symptoms or readmissions. MONARCA I showed a non-significant trend toward more sustained depressive symptoms, and subgroups in both trials had more depressive and fewer manic symptoms. Self-monitoring is valued by many users and can support early-warning detection. However, it is not a treatment, should be brief and simple, and needs attention to rumination and negative effects. People with BD value sharing data with trusted others when they control it. Sharing also raises concerns about autonomy, misinterpretation and privacy.

### Cited Findings
- **MONARCA I** (Faurholt-Jepsen et al., Psychol Med 2015; n=78; 6 months; daily smartphone self-monitoring with a clinical feedback loop vs ordinary phone) **[High; RCT, small]**:
  - Intention-to-treat analysis showed no significant effect on depressive (HAMD-17) or manic (YMRS) symptoms.
  - There was a trend toward more sustained depressive symptoms in the intervention group (B=2.02, 95% CI −0.13 to 4.17, p=0.066).
  - Subgroups had more depressive and fewer manic symptoms.
  - The authors say self-monitoring "needs critical consideration and further clarification before it is implemented as a clinical tool."
  - [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/identifier/S0033291715000410/type/journal_article); [PubMed 26220802](https://www.pubmed.gov/26220802/)
- **MONARCA II** (Psychol Med 2019; n=129) **[High; RCT]**:
  - Intention-to-treat analysis showed no significant effect on depressive or manic symptoms.
  - In sub-analyses, the intervention arm had a higher risk of depressive episodes and a lower risk of manic episodes.
  - [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/psychological-medicine/article/effect-of-smartphonebased-monitoring-on-illness-activity-in-bipolar-disorder-the-monarca-ii-randomized-controlled-singleblinded-trial/FDD01FC31A211390BA82C2DB0886E754)
- **RADMIS** (n=98) **[High; RCT]**: smartphone monitoring did not reduce the rate or duration of readmissions, and depressive symptoms did not differ. — [Journal of Bipolar Disorders PDF](https://journalbipolardisorders.springeropen.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s40345-020-00205-1); [Acta Psychiatr Scand (zendy)](https://zendy.io/title/10.1111/acps.13274)
- **SmartBipolar trial** (NCT04230421): results not retrieved. — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04230421)
- **UK focus groups with people with BD** (RADAR passive monitoring; n=17; JMIR Formative 2025) **[Low; qualitative]**:
  - Themes included sharing with family and friends and with professionals.
  - Users mainly want self-management insight and understanding of triggers.
  - Designers should not assume users want formal services involved.
  - Valued features: crisis plans and controlled data sharing.
  - [JMIR Formative 2025](https://formative.jmir.org/2025/1/e65140); [Bristol](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/improving-the-utility-safety-and-ethical-use-of-a-passive-mood-tr/)
- **Qualitative meta-synthesis on mood monitoring in BD** (JMIR 2025) **[Low–moderate]**:
  - Sharing data was seen as empowering when it enabled communication with trusted people or clinicians.
  - It also raised "concerns around autonomy, misinterpretation, and privacy."
  - Passive collection was seen as less intrusive and more sustainable.
  - [PMC11845880](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11845880/)
- **IPSRT's Social Rhythm Metric** records wake time, first contact, start of work, meals and bedtime. It is a validated low-burden structure for tracking regularity. — [Carlat Report](https://www.thecarlatreport.com/articles/4901-treating-bipolar-disorder-with-interpersonal-and-social-rhythm-therapy)

### Inferences
- Keep the diary minimal: one or two taps a day.
  - Mood on a bipolar scale from −3 (very low) to +3 (very high), with 0 as balanced. This mirrors the MONARCA-style −3 to +3 mood item; the exact MONARCA item format was not verified here.
  - Energy and activity level.
  - Sleep hours or bed and wake time.
  - Optional: medication taken (yes/no), irritability, and side effects (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea).
- Avoid long questionnaires and free-text prompts that invite rumination on bad days.
- Early-warning patterns worth surfacing gently, only to the user unless she opts in to sharing:
  - several nights of markedly reduced sleep with high energy;
  - several days of very low mood or energy;
  - a sudden spike in planned or performed exercise.
- These should prompt "consider contacting your care team or using your personal early-warning plan," not a diagnosis.
- Because MONARCA hinted that monitoring may sustain attention on depressive symptoms, the app should:
  - show trends sparingly;
  - avoid red, alarming visuals for low days;
  - let her hide history.
- **Son as admin (ethics):**
  - Her autonomy is paramount. She should explicitly choose, and be able to change at any time, what her son sees.
  - A sensible default: activity completed and general check-in only, with mood, weight and sleep shared only if she opts in.
  - She should see exactly what he can see ("viewing as Son" preview) and get a log of when he viewed it.
  - Shared data can be misinterpreted (meta-synthesis), so any family-facing view should avoid raw low-mood scores without context, or should be agreed in advance as part of a crisis plan.
  - The son should not become a monitor-enforcer of weight or exercise targets. The relationship should be supportive.
- An agreed "if I score X for Y days, it's OK to check in on me" plan, made when she is well (an advance-directive-style approach), fits the user-valued "crisis plans and controlled sharing" theme.

### Gaps
- MONARCA's exact item scales were not retrieved.
- No caregiver-focused qualitative study was retrieved, and no study specifically on adult children viewing a parent's mood data.
- No evidence-graded list of the best simple scales for apps was found. Social Rhythm Metric and Life-Chart-style ratings are established in clinical practice but were not verified here.

## 6. Language and design: shame, stigma, eating-disorder triggers, weight-neutral framing, obsessive tracking

### Takeaway
Diet and fitness apps that use calorie counting and frame weight loss as a proxy for health are associated with eating-disorder symptoms in susceptible users and can embed weight stigma through language, images and functions. The evidence is low (qualitative and cross-sectional), but consistent. A weight-neutral, behaviour- and wellbeing-focused framing is safer, especially combined with appetite-suppressing medication and mood instability.

### Cited Findings
- **Eikey 2021 qualitative study** (24 women with self-identified eating disorders) **[Low]**: diet and fitness apps can worsen eating-disorder behaviours, and "using weight loss as a proxy for health is problematic." — [PMC8485346](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8485346/); [National Elf Service](https://www.nationalelfservice.net/mental-health/eating-disorders/myfitnesspal-eating-disorders/)
- **Simpson & Mazzeo** (n=493 students) **[Low; cross-sectional]**: app users had higher eating-disorder symptoms. — cited in [Frontiers Comp Sci 2023](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2023.1215588/full)
- **Calorie tracking in people with eating disorders** **[Low; clinical sample]**: 73% of 105 people with eating disorders felt a calorie tracker contributed to their disorder. — [Frontiers Comp Sci 2023](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2023.1215588/full)
- **2023 expert evaluation of 10 free weight-loss apps** **[Low]**: some apps passively tolerate or actively encourage drastic weight loss through their design. — [Frontiers Comp Sci 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2023.1215588/pdf)
- **Weight stigma** "may be unintentionally embedded into health-focused apps via language, images, and functionality" (Obesity Science & Practice 2020) **[Low; commentary]**. — [PMC8019276](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8019276)
- **Analysis of 95 weight-loss apps** **[Low]**: their language links thinness with health and happiness, contributing to stigma. — [UIC Barcelona](https://www.uic.es/en/noticia/study-lecturer-lara-martin-vicario-says-weight-loss-apps-perpetuate-body-stereotypes)
- **Flinders University review** (38 studies) on apps and obsession with weight, body image and calorie counting. — [SBS](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/article/fit-or-fixated-could-health-apps-be-doing-more-harm-than-good/ydtjhgou3)

### Inferences
- Avoid:
  - calorie counting as a core feature;
  - daily weigh-in prompts;
  - "before/after" imagery;
  - leaderboards;
  - streak-loss penalties;
  - red "failed" states;
  - words like "cheat", "bad food", "obese", "fat-burning" or "earn your meal";
  - celebrating rapid loss.
- Mounjaro suppresses appetite. The app should never praise under-eating and should flag very low intake as a health and safety issue, given lithium and dehydration risk and nutrition needs.
- Weigh-ins should be optional and infrequent (e.g. weekly or less, or set with her clinician). Weight should be hideable from her own view and from the son's view.
- Hypomanic phases can bring impulsive or excessive dieting or exercise, and depressive phases bring self-criticism. Tone must work at both poles.

### Gaps
- No formal, evidence-based language style guide for weight apps was found. Weight-inclusive language guidance (e.g. from obesity societies or ED organisations) would need a targeted search.
- No study specifically examined ED risk in GLP-1 RA users of tracking apps.

## 7. Crisis safety: surfacing crisis resources (Sweden) non-intrusively, and app design implications

### Takeaway
Swedish crisis pathways:
- **112** for acute danger or suicide risk.
- **1177** for healthcare advice, including how to reach psychiatric emergency (psykiatrisk akutmottagning).
- **Mind Självmordslinjen**: phone 90101 and chat at mind.se. It is anonymous and free, and also open to people worried about someone else.

Sources conflict on Självmordslinjen's opening hours. Mind's own page says 24/7, while older sources say 06–24. Verify at mind.se before hard-coding. User-centred BD research supports built-in crisis plans and user-controlled sharing.

### Cited Findings
- 1177 lists Mind Självmordslinjen (90101, chat via mind.se) and the on-call priest (via 112). Psychiatric emergency receptions handle acute needs, otherwise call 1177. — [1177 stödlinjer](https://1177.se/stodlinjer); [1177 "Till dig som har självmordstankar"](https://www.1177.se/Gotland/sjukdomar--besvar/psykiska-sjukdomar-och-besvar/sjalvmordstankar/till-dig-som-har-sjalvmordstankar/)
- Mind (English page) describes Självmordslinjen as open 24 hours, every day. — [Mind.se](https://mind.se/en/are-you-experiencing-suicidal-thoughts/)
- Older sources give 06–24: [Sveriges Radio 2018](https://sverigesradio.se/artikel/6941742); [TV4](https://www.tv4.se/artikel/5e9e933d24bc623a83127d90/sjalvmordstankar-har-kan-du-soka-hjalp). This is a source conflict.
- At acute times, or with thoughts of suicide, call 112. — [TV4](https://www.tv4.se/artikel/5e9e933d24bc623a83127d90/sjalvmordstankar-har-kan-du-soka-hjalp)
- Självmordslinjen has been overloaded (about half a million missed calls in a year), so offer alternatives such as chat, 1177 and 112. — [TV4](https://www.tv4.se/artikel/CPNwP0naY2IVjC7KpR66w/sjaelvmordslinjen-nedringd-missade-en-halv-miljon-samtal-ifjol)
- People with BD value crisis plans and controlled data sharing in monitoring tools. — [JMIR Formative 2025](https://formative.jmir.org/2025/1/e65140)

### Inferences: App design implications

**A. Concrete features**

1. **Daily check-in (about 15 seconds).** Ask:
   - mood (−3 to +3);
   - energy (low, ok, high);
   - sleep (hours, or bed and wake time);
   - medication taken (yes/no, optional);
   - Mounjaro side effects (none, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation);
   - fluids (rough: "less than usual / usual").
   
   Allow skipping, and never penalise skipped days.
2. **Plan picker driven by the check-in.** The day's plan should come from the check-in, not from a fixed programme:
   - **"Heavy day"**: 2–5 minutes of gentle movement (seated stretches, a short walk to the window or mailbox, breathing). Make it explicit that rest counts.
   - **"Ordinary day"**: 10–20 minutes of low-impact moderate activity (walking, chair or water exercise, light resistance bands).
   - **"High-energy day"**: the same moderate plan, not more, plus a gentle note: "Lots of energy today. Keeping to your usual amount helps keep things steady." Do not unlock extra challenges on high-energy days.
3. **Anti-boom-bust progression.**
   - Hard cap on weekly increases, for example at most about 10–20% more minutes than the previous week. This is a pragmatic rule, not BD-specific evidence.
   - No streaks that "break." Use "days moved this month" or a cumulative count.
   - After a gap, use a "welcome back" restart at a comfortable level with no catch-up.
   - Track rhythm consistency (same time of day) as the key success metric, in line with IPSRT.
4. **Routine anchoring.**
   - Let her choose a fixed daily "movement time", ideally morning or daytime, with daylight exposure where possible.
   - Show regular bed and wake time as a supportive goal.
   - Suggest avoiding vigorous activity in the last 1–2 hours before bed. This is a precautionary inference, not BD evidence.
5. **Early-warning patterns (private by default).** Example triggers:
   - 2 or more nights of much less sleep plus high energy or mood +2/+3;
   - a sudden doubling of activity;
   - 5 or more days at mood −2/−3;
   - skipped medication for several days.
   
   Response: a calm card saying "You might want to look at your early-warning plan or contact your care team," with her own pre-written plan (written when well) and contacts.
6. **Personal crisis plan.** Created with her (and optionally her care team) when she is well:
   - her personal warning signs;
   - what helps;
   - whom to contact;
   - what the son may do (e.g. "call me", "contact my psychiatric team").
7. **Lithium-aware hydration module.** Activated only if she indicates that she takes lithium. Do not assume; ask once and allow "I don't know."
   - Daily fluids reminder, with extra prompts on exercise days, hot days and side-effect days.
   - **Warning prompt** if she logs vomiting, diarrhoea or very low food or fluids: "Because you take lithium, vomiting/diarrhoea or not drinking enough can raise lithium levels. Please contact your clinic/1177 today. If you feel confused, very unsteady, have coarse shaking or severe drowsiness, call 112."
   - **Reminder at Mounjaro start and each dose step**: "Ask your prescriber whether you need an extra lithium blood test." Basis: case reports, plus expert advice to check before starting and about 5 days after.
   - Reminder to tell every prescriber (GP, obesity clinic, psychiatry) that she takes both drugs.
8. **General medication-safety prompts (all users).**
   - If she vomits soon after taking oral medicines repeatedly, or misses several days of lamotrigine, prompt her to contact her clinic.
   - Never suggest changing psychiatric medication to aid weight loss.
   - Remind her that Mounjaro can affect absorption of some tablets at start and dose increases, so she should tell her doctor about all medicines (per the EU SmPC).
9. **Nutrition safety under appetite suppression.**
   - Prompt protein, fluids and regular small meals.
   - Flag "ate very little for several days" as a care issue, not an achievement.
10. **Optional, infrequent weight logging.** Weekly or less, hideable, with trend smoothing. No goal weight on the home screen. Non-scale wins (stairs, sleep, mood stability, walking distance, blood pressure, how clothes fit) should be equally prominent.

**B. Tone guidelines**
- Use warm, plain and permission-giving language:
  - "Rest is part of the plan."
  - "Any movement counts."
  - "Welcome back."
  - "Some mood medicines make weight loss slower. That's the medicine, not you."
- Use person-first, weight-neutral wording: "body", "health", "strength", "energy". Avoid "obese", "fat", "cheat", "bad", "failed", "should" and "no excuses".
- Celebrate consistency and self-care, not intensity or kilos lost.
- On low days, be brief and quiet. Avoid toxic positivity and long reflective prompts that may feed rumination.
- On high-energy days, be calm and steadying, not hype-y. No "beast mode", fireworks or "go further" nudges.
- Write in Swedish for this user, with simple wording and no clinical jargon.

**C. What to avoid**
- Calorie counting as a core feature, daily weigh-in nudges, before/after photos, leaderboards, social comparison and rapid-loss celebrations.
- Breakable streaks, guilt notifications ("You missed 3 days!"), red failure states and catch-up plans.
- Escalating challenges or "programmes" that ramp intensity automatically, which can reinforce the 1–2-week all-in cycle.
- Any diagnostic language, such as "You are becoming manic." Use pattern descriptions and suggestions instead.
- Late-night engagement nudges or notifications that disrupt sleep regularity. Give notifications quiet hours.
- Interpreting GLP-1 side effects as success ("great, you're not hungry!").

**D. Crisis resources (non-intrusive)**
- Keep a permanent but low-key "Need support now?" link in the menu or footer. Do not place it on every screen in a way that feels alarming. It should list:
  - **112**: acute danger or immediate risk;
  - **1177**: healthcare advice and how to reach psychiatric emergency;
  - **Mind Självmordslinjen, 90101 / chat at mind.se**, with opening hours verified before launch;
  - her own care-team contacts and personal crisis plan.
- Surface it proactively only:
  - when she scores the lowest mood for several days;
  - when she uses an optional "I'm having thoughts of harming myself" or "I need help" button;
  - with the lithium toxicity symptoms above.
- Use one calm card with tap-to-call, not a modal she must dismiss repeatedly.
- Do not imply the son or the app is monitoring for suicide risk in real time unless that is truly the case and agreed. False reassurance is a safety risk.

**E. Privacy and the son-as-admin role**
- Consent:
  - Get explicit, granular, revocable consent from her for each data category the son can see: activity, check-in completed, mood, energy, sleep, weight, side effects, crisis-plan alerts.
  - Recommended default: activity and "checked in today" only.
  - Mood, weight and free-text notes are private unless she opts in.
- Transparency:
  - a "what my son sees" preview;
  - a viewing log, or a notification when shared data is viewed;
  - a periodic (e.g. 3-monthly) "still OK with these sharing settings?" check, made at a calm moment.
- The son should not have rights to edit her entries, change her plans to be harder, or turn off safety features. An admin role for technical setup should be separate from data visibility.
- Agree in advance, when she is well, what pattern (if any) triggers a notification to him, and what he should do (e.g. "call me", not "confront me about weight").
- Data protection:
  - Health data is special-category data under GDPR. Store it minimally, encrypt it, and avoid third-party analytics SDKs on health screens.
  - Allow export and deletion.
  - Do not use mood data for marketing or engagement optimisation.
- Offer a "pause sharing" button she can use at any time, including during episodes, without having to explain. Plan in advance with her how this interacts with any agreed crisis plan.

### Gaps
- Mind Självmordslinjen's current opening hours conflict across sources and must be verified at mind.se before launch.
- I found no evaluation of crisis-resource placement in BD apps, so the placement advice is inferential.
- I found no Swedish regulatory guidance on whether such an app would count as a medical device (MDR) once it includes early-warning or lithium-toxicity prompts. This should be checked, because symptom-based warnings may push it toward medical-device classification.
- Several numerical thresholds (weekly progression cap, number of days for early-warning triggers, hydration targets) are pragmatic suggestions without direct evidence. They should be agreed with her psychiatric team, especially any lithium-related fluid advice.
