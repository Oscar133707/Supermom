## 1. Pre-exercise screening (ACSM algorithm), medical clearance and messaging

### Takeaway
Under ACSM's 2015 screening algorithm, the decision rests on three factors: whether she exercises now, whether she has known or symptomatic cardiovascular/metabolic/renal disease, and how hard she plans to exercise. An inactive person with no known disease and no symptoms can start light-to-moderate exercise without clearance. **However**, at BMI ~55 with untested metabolic status, a new prescription drug and psychiatric medication, the sensible message is that she should check with her doctor (who is already prescribing Mounjaro), while being told that starting gentle activity is low-risk and encouraged. The check is a reasonable precaution, not a barrier.

### Cited Findings
- ACSM updated its pre-participation screening in Nov 2015 (Riebe et al., *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 2015;47(11):2473–2479) to remove unnecessary barriers. The older CVD risk-factor-count approach was judged overly complicated and caused excessive physician referrals — [ACE summary](https://www.acefitness.org/education-and-resources/professional/expert-articles/6921/new-preparticipation-screening-guidelines-what-health-and-fitness-pros-need-to-know/); [ACSM blog](https://www.acsm.org/blog-detail/acsm-certified-blog/2018/02/01/exercise-preparticipation-screening-removing-barriers-initiating-exercise) (title/snippet only; page not fetchable). *Evidence: expert consensus (ACSM).*
- The algorithm uses three factors: (1) current exercise participation, (2) known cardiovascular, metabolic or renal disease, or signs/symptoms of it, and (3) desired exercise intensity. Risk-factor scores were removed because they do not reliably predict exercise-related cardiac events — [NFPT](https://nfpt.com/assessing-cardiovascular-risk-with-fitness-clients/); [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/pros/expert-articles/6921/new-preparticipation-screening-guidelines-what-health-and-fitness-pros-need-to-know/).
- Anyone with signs or symptoms suggestive of these diseases should stop exercising and seek clearance, whatever their known disease status. Regular exercisers without symptoms can continue moderate exercise without clearance — [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/pros/expert-articles/6921/new-preparticipation-screening-guidelines-what-health-and-fitness-pros-need-to-know/).
- Applied to NHANES data, the new algorithm referred ~41% fewer people to a physician than the old one (Whitfield et al., 2017) — [NFPT](https://nfpt.com/assessing-cardiovascular-risk-with-fitness-clients/).
- The UK Moving Medicine risk consensus says the risk of cardiac chest pain during exercise is very low but rises with age and intensity. People should be counselled individually to increase activity gradually — [Moving Medicine risk consensus](https://movingmedicine.ac.uk/riskconsensus). *Evidence: expert consensus (UK).*
- A US hospital patient guide lists obesity among the conditions to review with a clinician before starting an exercise programme — [UH Hospitals](https://www.uhhospitals.org/rainbow/services/pediatric-developmental-and-behavioral-issues/conditions-and-treatments/article/adult-diseases-and-conditions-v0/exercise-before-starting-an-exercise-program). *Low.*
- Obesity Canada's activities-of-daily-living chapter recommends that providers assess fall risk, since falls can limit ability and willingness to be active — [Obesity Canada ADL chapter](https://obesitycanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/5-Activities-of-Daily-Life-v5-with-links.pdf) (search snippet).

### Inferences
- Under the strict algorithm, an inactive person with no known CVD, metabolic or renal disease and no symptoms needs no medical clearance for light-to-moderate exercise. Obesity alone is not one of the algorithm's "disease" categories. **[Algorithm detail from background knowledge of Riebe 2015; matches the three-factor structure cited above.]**
- Practical caveats for this user:
  - At BMI ~55 the chance of undiagnosed type 2 diabetes, hypertension or sleep apnoea is high.
  - Breathlessness on stairs is probably deconditioning, but the doctor should confirm it is not a "symptom" in the algorithm's sense (e.g. disproportionate dyspnoea).
  - She is starting a GLP-1/GIP drug and takes bipolar medication.

  Since she will see the Mounjaro prescriber anyway, the low-friction recommendation is: "Start the gentle run-in now (walking around the home, chair exercises, easy bike at RPE ≤3/10), and at the next appointment mention the plan and ask: any reason to avoid moderate exercise? Should blood pressure, glucose or ECG be checked? Do any of my medications affect heat tolerance, hydration or heart rate?"
- Messaging should be non-alarmist and weight-neutral: "some is better than none" (WHO). Clearance is framed as a routine check, not a sign of danger.

### Gaps
- Full text of Riebe 2015 and the ACSM GETP 11th/12th edition algorithm figure could not be retrieved (acsm.org blocked). Exact box wording ("light to moderate", "progress gradually as tolerated") is from background knowledge.
- No retrieved source gave obesity-class-specific screening advice (e.g. a BMI threshold for an ECG or exercise test).
- **Medication interactions to check with the prescriber [UNVERIFIED, background knowledge]:**
  - If she takes **lithium**, dehydration (heat, sweating, or GLP-1-related vomiting/diarrhoea/reduced intake) can raise lithium levels toward toxicity. This is a real safety point to raise.
  - Some antipsychotics impair heat regulation.
  - Some mood stabilisers and antipsychotics affect weight, sedation and balance.

  No retrieved source covered this; flag it for the medication/psychiatry researcher.

## 2. Recommended starting volume, frequency and intensity (aerobic + resistance) in severe obesity; RPE and talk test

### Takeaway
Guidelines agree on long-term targets: ≥150 min/week of moderate aerobic activity (more, ~200–300 min, for weight-loss maintenance), muscle-strengthening on ≥2 days/week, and less sedentary time. They also agree that inactive people should start well below these targets and build up gradually. For someone out of breath on stairs, "moderate" in absolute terms may be impossible at first. Intensity should therefore be set *relative* to her current capacity, using RPE and the talk test.

### Cited Findings
- **Obesity Canada CPG, Physical Activity chapter (Boulé & Prud'homme, v1 Aug 2020; appears still current):**
  - Physical activity benefits adults with obesity in all BMI categories even without weight loss.
  - Aerobic: 30–60 min of moderate-to-vigorous activity on most days, accumulating ≥150 min/week.
  - Resistance training: ≥2 days/week.
  - Reduce sedentary time.
  - Brisk walking is the most commonly prescribed activity.

  Sources: [Obesity Canada PA chapter](https://obesitycanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Physical-Activity-in-Obesity-Management.pdf) (search summary; PDF not fetchable); [CMAJ guideline summary](https://www.cmaj.ca/content/cmaj/192/31/E875.full.pdf). *Evidence: national CPG (GRADE-based).*
- **ACSM Position Stand 2009 (Donnelly et al., *MSSE* 41(2):459–471):**
  - 150–250 min/week of moderate activity prevents weight gain and gives only modest weight loss.
  - More than 250 min/week gives clinically significant weight loss.
  - Some evidence that more than 250 min/week helps prevent regain.
  - 150–250 min/week improves weight loss only when combined with *moderate* (not severe) diet restriction.

  Sources: [PubMed 19127177](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19127177/); [Obesity Medicine Association summary](https://obesitymedicine.org/how-much-physical-activity-is-needed-for-weight-loss-weight-loss-maintenance-and-weight-gain-prevention/). *Evidence: position stand based on graded literature review.*
- **WHO 2020 Guidelines (Bull et al., *BJSM* 2020, doi:10.1136/bjsports-2020-102955):**
  - Regular muscle-strengthening activity is recommended for all age groups (≥2 days/week for adults, carried over from 2010).
  - The previous ≥10-minute minimum bout length was dropped; "some physical activity is better than none".

  Sources: [QUB repository](https://pure.qub.ac.uk/en/publications/world-health-organization-2020-guidelines-on-physical-activity-an/); [Inverse report](https://www.inverse.com/mind-body/world-health-organization-exercise-definition). *Evidence: international guideline (GRADE).*
- **Systematic reviews in class II/III obesity:**
  - Baillot et al. 2015 (*PLoS ONE*): 56 studies, ~80% uncontrolled, of lifestyle interventions that included physical activity in class II/III obesity. Pooled weight loss was 8.9 kg, with significant effects on fat mass, waist circumference, blood pressure, lipids and fasting insulin. The authors say higher-quality trials are needed — [EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:plo:pone00:0119017). *Moderate–low (mostly uncontrolled designs).*
  - Baillot et al. 2014 (*Obes Rev*): reviewed 40 papers on physical activity and fitness in class II/III obesity and bariatric patients — [UQO repository](https://di.uqo.ca/1933/1/Baillot%2C%20A.%20et%20al.%20%282014%29.%20Impact%20of%20physical%20activity%20and%20fitness%20in%20class%20II%20and%20III%20obese%20individuals%20-%20A%20SR.pdf) (findings not retrieved).
  - A systematic review of BMI ≥40 trials (PEDro-rated) concluded programmes were effective regardless of training modality — [PMC12884621](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12884621/) (snippet).
- **ACSM GETP:** the 11th edition has an overweight/obesity FITT box, and a **12th edition** has now been released with an updated resistance-training chapter (Ch. 5) — [ACE update summary](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/pros/expert-articles/8996/updates-to-acsm-s-guidelines-for-exercise-testing-and-prescription/); [ACSM 12e page](https://www.acsm.org/education-resources/books/guidelines-exercise-testing-prescription). Specific FITT numbers could not be retrieved.
- **Preferences in severe obesity:** people prefer walking, cycling and swimming at moderate intensity. Pain, poor health and depression are the main barriers, and addressing them improves adherence — [PMC12884621 / related review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12884621/) (snippet). *Low.*

### Inferences
- Intensity anchors for her plan (the RPE ↔ %HR mapping is standard practice **[UNVERIFIED here]**):
  - **Borg CR10:** 2–3 = light; 3–4 = moderate ("can talk in full sentences but not sing"); 5–6 = somewhat hard / upper moderate; ≥7 = vigorous.
  - **Borg 6–20:** ~11–12 = light-moderate; 12–14 = moderate; 15+ = hard.
  - **Talk test:** moderate = can talk but not sing; too hard = cannot finish a sentence.
- Heart-rate targets are less reliable here (deconditioning, possible medications), so **RPE + talk test should be primary**.
- Weeks 1–3 should aim at RPE 2–4/10 (CR10), i.e. "can chat comfortably". Total accumulated time can start at ~5–10 min/day.
- Resistance training should start at 1 set of 8–15 reps at RPE ~4–6/10, leaving 3–4 reps "in the tank", on 2 non-consecutive days/week. Build towards 2–3 sets on 2–3 days/week.

### Gaps
- Exact ACSM GETP 11th/12th edition obesity FITT values were not verified. Commonly cited values are aerobic ≥5 days/wk, moderate (40–59% HRR) progressing to vigorous, 30→60 min/day, 150→250–300 min/wk; resistance 2–3 days/wk at 60–70% 1RM, 8–12 reps, 2–4 sets **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **NICE** obesity guidance (NG246, 2025, or CG189) physical-activity wording was not retrieved.
- **EASO** physical-activity guidance (e.g. Oppert et al. 2021 EASO Physical Activity Working Group statement) was not retrieved.

## 3. Low-impact modalities: walking, stationary cycling, chair-based, elastic bands, water

### Takeaway
- **Elastic-band training** gives strength gains comparable to conventional free-weight/machine training (meta-analysis; moderate evidence). Her band kit is a valid primary tool, not a compromise.
- **Walking** and **cycling** are the preferred and best-supported aerobic modalities in severe obesity.
- **Stationary cycling** offloads body weight, which makes it the ideal early aerobic base, *provided the bike's user-weight rating is ≥ her weight*.
- **Chair-based work** removes balance and fall risk.
- **Water exercise** offers the most unloading but requires leaving home.

### Cited Findings
- **Elastic bands = free weights for strength.** Lopes et al. 2019, *SAGE Open Med* 7:2050312119831116 (PMID 30815258), systematic review/meta-analysis of 8 studies (searched to Dec 2017):
  - Upper-limb strength SMD −0.011 (95% CI −0.40 to 0.19; p=0.48).
  - Lower-limb strength SMD 0.09 (95% CI −0.18 to 0.35; p=0.52).
  - Conclusion: "elastic resistance training is able to promote similar strength gains to conventional resistance training" across varied populations.

  Sources: [PMC6383082](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6383082); [PubMed](https://www.pubmed.gov/30815258/). *Moderate (small number of heterogeneous studies).*
  - **Caveat:** a separate 2020 meta-analysis from the same group (*Fisioterapia em Movimento*) on **body composition** reported better outcomes from conventional devices — [PUCPR](https://periodicos.pucpr.br/fisio/article/download/26631/24148/53049). So bands match free weights for strength, but evidence on muscle mass and body composition favours conventional loading. Progressively heavier bands and/or dumbbells are likely needed over time.
- **Walking biomechanics in severe obesity:** shorter strides, slower speed, wider stance, and reduced joint range of motion at slower speeds. The review emphasises core training to offset this — [PMC12884621](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12884621/). In adults with mean BMI 41.5, more pain sites were associated with slower walking speed (SPPB) — same source. *Low.*
- **Joint load during walking:** consumer and expert estimates suggest roughly 2–7× body weight passes through the knee per walking step (often quoted as ~3×) — [Arthritis-Health](https://arthritis-health.com/treatment/diet-and-nutrition/how-effective-weight-loss-treating-knee-arthritis-pain). *Very low/low (secondary).* See section 6 for weight-loss effects.
- **Physiotherapy:** obesity physio tip sheets cover walking difficulty, getting out of a chair, and assistive devices, and support referral for movement limitations and pain — [Alberta Health Services tip sheet](https://albertahealthservices.ca/assets/info/hp/phc/if-hp-phc-obesity-pt-tip-sheet.pdf).
- Brisk walking is the activity most commonly prescribed for obesity — [Obesity Canada PA chapter](https://obesitycanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Physical-Activity-in-Obesity-Management.pdf) (search summary).

### Inferences
- **Stationary bike (practical; [UNVERIFIED] engineering norms):**
  - **Weight rating:** many budget upright home bikes are rated for only ~100–130 kg. At 140 kg, check the label or manual. If it is under ~140–150 kg, use it cautiously or consider a recumbent or heavy-duty bike (often rated 150–180 kg) later.
  - **Seat height:** at the bottom of the pedal stroke, the knee is slightly bent (~25–35°) with the ball of the foot on the pedal.
  - **Upright vs recumbent:** an upright bike may be uncomfortable because the abdomen meets the thighs at the top of the stroke. Raising the handlebars or setting the seat slightly higher/further back can help. A recumbent bike is often more comfortable at this size.
  - **Saddle comfort:** a wider, gel or noseless saddle cover and padded cycling shorts reduce saddle pain and chafing.
  - **Starting dose:** 3–5 min at low resistance and RPE 2–3, 1–3×/day. Add 1–2 min per session per week before raising resistance.
- **Walking at home:**
  - She is home all day, so indoor "walking laps", walking during TV ads and short outdoor walks are realistic.
  - Supportive, cushioned, wide-toe-box shoes, possibly a wide fitting.
  - Start with 3–5 min bouts at a comfortable pace, then use intervals (e.g. 2 min easy / 1 min brisker) once 10 min is easy.
- **Chair-based work** fits week 1 and any bad day (low mood, nausea, joint pain): seated marching, seated band rows, seated knee extension, seated adduction squeeze.
- **Water walking or aqua-aerobics** is a good optional add-on when she can travel to a pool. It relieves joint loading and heat stress.

### Gaps
- No retrieved source gave bike-fit or weight-capacity recommendations specific to large bodies, or a home cycling protocol for BMI >50.
- No retrieved aquatic RCT in severe obesity. No footwear evidence.
- The Lopes 2019 full text (included populations, risk of bias) was not read.

## 4. Specific safe exercises per muscle group, with regressions/progressions and floor considerations

### Takeaway
No retrieved guideline gives a prescriptive exercise list for BMI >50. The selection below follows general resistance-training principles: big multi-joint movements first, loads she can control, chair and wall support, and floor work only once she can get up and down safely. Each exercise is chosen to be possible at 140 kg with her equipment. The evidence base for *specific* exercise choices in this population is **very low (practice-based)**. The evidence that progressive resistance training builds and preserves muscle during weight loss is **moderate–high** (see sections 5 and 7).

### Cited Findings
- In older adults with obesity on calorie restriction, resistance training reduced lean-mass loss compared with aerobic training alone, in a 160-participant body of evidence — [PMC11981955](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11981955/); [scoping review PMC10393929](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10393929/). *Moderate.*
- Core training is recommended in severe obesity to offset gait and stability limitations — [PMC12884621](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12884621/). *Low.*
- Physiotherapy guidance includes practising functional transfers such as rising from a chair — [AHS tip sheet](https://albertahealthservices.ca/assets/info/hp/phc/if-hp-phc-obesity-pt-tip-sheet.pdf). *Low.*
- The T-REx trial program "starts with light-to-moderate intensity to build technique" and follows Exercise & Sport Science Australia resistance-training recommendations — [ANZCTR ACTRN12623001236684](https://anzctr.org.au/ACTRN12623001236684.aspx). *Moderate (RCT protocol).*

### Inferences (proposed exercise menu; practice-based, not trial-tested in BMI 55)

| Target | Base exercise | Regression | Progression |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legs/glutes (quads, glutes) | **Sit-to-stand** from sturdy chair | Higher seat (cushion), hands push on thighs or armrests, partial range | No hands → lower seat → slow 3-s lowering → hold dumbbell(s) at chest → mini band above knees → split-stance sit-to-stand |
| Glutes | **Standing glute squeeze / standing hip extension with loop band** (hand on chair) | No band | Heavier mini band; later **glute bridge** on floor/bed (bed bridge avoids getting down to the floor) → band above knees → single-leg-biased bridge |
| Glutes (side/abductors) | **Seated band abduction** (mini band above knees, push knees out) | Light band | Standing side-step with band, standing hip abduction holding chair |
| Inner thighs (adductors) | **Seated adduction squeeze** (pillow/ball or folded towel between knees, 5-s holds) | Softer squeeze, shorter hold | Side-lying adduction (when floor or bed work is OK); standing band adduction (band anchored at door); wider-stance sit-to-stand (sumo) |
| Back (lats/rhomboids) | **Seated band row** (band around feet or anchored at door) | Light band, one arm | Standing row → slower tempo → heavier band; **band pull-apart** for upper back; one-arm dumbbell row with hand on chair |
| Chest/shoulders/triceps | **Wall push-up** | Closer to the wall, smaller range | Feet further back → kitchen-counter incline push-up → sturdy table → (much later) knee push-up |
| Shoulders | **Seated dumbbell or band shoulder press** (1–3 kg) | One arm at a time, partial range | Standing press, heavier load |
| Biceps | **Seated dumbbell or band curl** | 1 kg | 2–5 kg, slower lowering |
| Triceps ("bingo wings") | **Seated overhead dumbbell triceps extension** or **band press-down** (door anchor) | Band, partial range | Heavier dumbbell; **chair dips** only much later (high shoulder load relative to body weight) |
| Core | **Seated marching with braced trunk; standing Pallof press with band; dead bug on bed** | Seated brace and breath only | Standing Pallof → tall-kneeling (when floor OK) → bird-dog → forearm plank against wall → incline plank on counter |
| Calves/ankles (running prep) | **Double-leg calf raise holding chair** | Seated calf raise | Slow lowering → single-leg calf raise (key running criterion) |
| Balance | **Supported tandem stand/single-leg stand at counter** | Feet hip-width, eyes open | Less hand support, longer holds |

- **Floor considerations:**
  - Getting up and down is "possible but taxing", so build floor transfers in as a *skill* rather than relying on floor exercises early.
  - Use the bed for bridges, dead bugs and side-lying adduction during months 1–3.
  - Practise a "floor transfer" drill 1–2×/week from ~month 2, using a chair for support. This also supports fall preparedness, which matters given the Obesity Canada focus on fall risk.
- **Inner thighs and loose skin:** strength training builds muscle beneath the skin, but evidence that exercise prevents or reduces loose skin after large weight loss is lacking. Messaging should be honest: muscle improves shape, function and body composition, but skin outcomes depend mainly on the amount and speed of loss, age and genetics **[UNVERIFIED — no source retrieved]**.
- **Breathing and blood pressure:** exhale on effort and avoid long breath-holds (Valsalva) **[standard practice, UNVERIFIED here]**.

### Gaps
- No RCT or guideline retrieved specifying exercise selection for BMI >50.
- No evidence retrieved on loose skin and exercise.

## 5. Progression rules over months; recovery; short bouts / "exercise snacks"

### Takeaway
Short accumulated bouts give health effects similar to one continuous session (meta-analysis, moderate–high). WHO 2020 removed the 10-minute minimum, so 2–5-minute "snacks" spread through her day at home fully count. For resistance training, double progression (add reps within a range, then add resistance) with 48 h between sessions for the same muscles is standard practice. For aerobic work, increasing weekly time by ~10% (or 1–2 min per bout) and changing one variable at a time is a conservative convention (low evidence; mainly expert practice and running-injury tradition).

### Cited Findings
- Murphy et al. 2019, *Sports Med* (19 studies, 1,080 participants), compared accumulated vs continuous exercise of the same total duration, mode and intensity:
  - No differences in cardiorespiratory fitness or blood pressure.
  - Body mass changes slightly favoured accumulated exercise (MD −0.92 kg, 95% CI −1.59 to −0.25; 5 studies).
  - LDL fell more with accumulated exercise in 2 small studies.
  - Conclusion: adults get similar benefits from one bout or several shorter bouts.

  Source: [PMC6745307](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6745307). *Moderate–high.*
- WHO 2020 removed the ≥10-min bout requirement; "any amount is better than none" — [Inverse/WHO coverage](https://www.inverse.com/mind-body/world-health-organization-exercise-definition); [Bull et al. repository](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/312925).
- A separate, newer systematic review/meta-analysis on "exercise snacks" (short vigorous bursts) states it was the first of its kind. Its results were not checked — [PMC12354995](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12354995/).
- **Resistance training dose during GLP-1 therapy:** the T-REx RCT used 3 × ~60-min supervised sessions per week for 40 weeks, starting at light-to-moderate intensity — [ANZCTR](https://anzctr.org.au/ACTRN12623001236684.aspx); [UWA press release](https://www.uwa.edu.au/news/article/2026/november/resistance-training-key-to-maintaining-muscle-function-during-glp1-weight-loss). Practitioner sources suggest 2–4 sessions/week of 30–45 min — [NFPT](https://nfpt.com/resistance-training-programming-for-clients-on-glp-1s) (*very low*).
- Moving Medicine: increase activity gradually, considering symptom severity and fear — [Moving Medicine](https://movingmedicine.ac.uk/riskconsensus).

### Inferences
- **Double progression (resistance):**
  - Pick a rep range (e.g. 10–15).
  - When all sets reach the top of the range at RPE ≤6–7 with good form on two consecutive sessions, increase resistance: next band colour, +0.5–1 kg dumbbell, or a harder regression level from the table above.
  - Reps then drop back to the bottom of the range.
- **Adding sets:** go from 1 → 2 sets per exercise once sessions feel routine (~week 4–6) and there is no excess soreness more than 48 h later. Go from 2 → 3 sets at ~3–4 months.
- **Aerobic progression:** add 1–2 min per bout, or one extra bout per day, per week. Keep weekly total increases around ≤10–20% at low baseline volumes (at 30 min/week, 10% is only 3 min, so absolute increments are more practical early). Raise duration before intensity.
- **Recovery:** resistance on non-consecutive days (e.g. Mon/Wed/Fri or Tue/Fri). Walking and easy cycling can be daily. Plan a lighter "deload" week every 4–6 weeks (≈50–60% volume), and after illness, GLP-1 dose escalation with significant nausea, or a mood episode.
- **Bad-day rule:** on low-energy, nausea or low-mood days, do the "minimum dose": 1 round of 3–4 chair exercises plus a 5-minute walk. Keeping the habit going matters more than the training load.

### Gaps
- No retrieved trial tested specific progression rules (e.g. 10% rule) in severe obesity. The "10% rule" mainly comes from running-injury lore and has weak evidence **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- T-REx sets, reps and load progression were not reported in retrieved sources.

## 6. Staged pathway from walking to eventual jogging (joint loading, criteria, timeline)

### Takeaway
Running is a realistic **long-term** goal but not a 12-month-certain one. Knee load per step in walking is already a multiple of body weight and is higher in running. Each kg lost reduces peak knee load by about 2–4 kg. Strength-based readiness criteria from physiotherapy return-to-run protocols are a better gate than a fixed BMI:
- 30 min of brisk pain-free walking
- 15–25 single-leg calf raises per side
- ~15 single-leg sit-to-stands per side
- 60-s controlled single-leg squats
- (later) pain-free hopping

A run-walk (Couch-to-5K-style) program with very short run intervals can start once these are met. Plausibly this is in the 9–15+ month window for her, depending on weight loss and strength gains.

### Cited Findings
- **Weight loss and knee load:**
  - Messier et al. 2005 (Wake Forest): each 1 lb lost reduced knee load by ~4 lb, accumulating to >4,800 lb less knee load per mile walked — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/pub_releases/2005-06/jws-tio062805.php). *Moderate (modelled).*
  - A 2011 study of obese adults with knee OA losing ~13.5% body weight found peak knee load fell 2.2 kg per kg lost after adjusting for walking speed. Reviews describe 2–4× reductions per kg lost — [core.ac.uk](https://core.ac.uk/download/492550375.pdf); [PMC6949016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6949016).
  - Some people **increased** joint loading after large weight loss, because they walked faster and moved more freely — [PMC6949016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6949016).
  - No longitudinal human evidence shows weight loss slows knee OA progression (Messier) — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/pub_releases/2005-06/jws-tio062805.php).
- **Walking load:** ~2–7× body weight per step depending on source, often quoted as ~3× — [Arthritis-Health](https://arthritis-health.com/treatment/diet-and-nutrition/how-effective-weight-loss-treating-knee-arthritis-pain). Knee loading rises in proportion to body weight, and running loads are higher still — [JYU thesis](https://jyx.jyu.fi/bitstream/123456789/46123/1/URN%3aNBN%3afi%3ajyu-201505292105.pdf). *Low.*
- **Physio return-to-run criteria** (designed for post-injury populations, extrapolated here):
  - 30 min brisk pain-free walking on consecutive days with no pain or swelling — [Pabau](https://pabau.com/blog/return-to-running-protocol-physical-therapy/).
  - Single-leg calf raise 20 reps/side and single-leg sit-to-stand 15 reps/side — [Physiotutors Return to Running Guidance](https://study.physiotutors.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Return-To-Running-Guidance.pdf).
  - More than 25 single-leg heel raises to metronome with full range and no pain, plus single-leg squat for 60 s/side with tempo, knee alignment and pain-free control — [Sanford Health ready-to-run](https://WWW.SANFORDHEALTH.ORG/-/media/org/files/medical-professionals/resources-and-education/return-to-running.pdf).
  - Hop tests are usually judged against the other limb (≥90%) — [NHS Scotland](https://clinicalguidelines.scot.nhs.uk/rhc-for-health-professionals/research-and-education-at-rhc/anterior-cruciate-ligament-acl-reconstruction-post-operative-rehabilitation-program/return-to-sport-test).
  - *Low (clinical protocols, not validated for obesity).*

### Inferences
- **Worked example:** losing 25 kg (140→115 kg) cuts peak knee load per walking step by roughly 55–100 kg, which is meaningful before any running starts.
- **Staged pathway:**
  1. Walking volume first: 30 continuous min brisk and pain-free, ≥3–5×/week.
  2. Strength gates: the calf-raise and single-leg sit-to-stand criteria above, plus a pain-free 60-s single-leg balance.
  3. Impact preparation: brisk walking intervals, step-ups, then low "pogo" hops on two feet holding a counter (only after the gates are met, and with no knee or ankle pain).
  4. Run-walk: e.g. 30 s very easy jog / 2–4 min walk × 6–8, 3×/week on non-consecutive days. Progress only when the previous week was pain-free with no next-day joint swelling.
  5. Adapted Couch-to-5K, repeating weeks freely.
- **Running pace:** an extremely slow "jog" (barely faster than walking), on smooth even surfaces, in supportive shoes, with music for motivation.
- **Timeline:** realistic only from roughly months 9–12 at the earliest. More likely, the first continuous jogging happens in year 2. Running should be framed as a milestone reached through gates, not a deadline.

### Gaps
- No guideline or BMI threshold for starting running in obesity was found. No study of running onset in people with class III obesity was found.
- Couch-to-5K adaptations for obesity were not retrieved.

## 7. Safety: warning signs, GLP-1 (tirzepatide) considerations, heat/hydration, chafing/skin, bipolar disorder

### Takeaway
- **Stop immediately and seek urgent care** for chest pain or pressure, fainting, severe or unusual breathlessness, irregular heartbeat, or dizziness.
- **Resistance training during tirzepatide treatment preserves muscle:** in the 2026 T-REx RCT it cut lean-tissue loss by ~25% and raised strength while non-exercisers lost strength. This makes resistance training a core part of her plan, not optional.
- **Hypoglycaemia** risk from tirzepatide alone is low; it rises mainly when combined with insulin or sulfonylureas [UNVERIFIED].
- **Practical risks** are low energy intake, nausea, dehydration and heat.
- **Bipolar disorder:** favour a regular routine at moderate intensity and watch for over-exercising as a possible early mania sign.

### Cited Findings
- **Warning signs:**
  - Stop and seek urgent medical help for chest pain, fainting/blackouts, severe or unusual breathlessness, or a sudden increase in breathlessness or dizziness — [Moving Medicine (everybody)](https://everybody.movingmedicine.ac.uk/symptom/palpitations/).
  - Stop, sit down and don't resume that session if dizzy, chest pain or irregular heartbeat occurs. Resume the next day only if symptoms have resolved — [Bradford Teaching Hospitals NHS](https://bradfordhospitals.nhs.uk/?p=35704). *Expert/clinical guidance.*
- **T-REx RCT (Nature Medicine, Nov 2026):**
  - Design: 120 adults aged 50–70 (men and postmenopausal women) with overweight/obesity on tirzepatide, randomised to 3×/week supervised resistance training for 40 weeks vs usual care.
  - Results: resistance training reduced loss of total and appendicular lean soft tissue (~25% less lean loss). Strength rose ~10% with training but fell ~10% without it, despite similar ~22 kg weight loss.
  - Funding: the drug maker supplied medication but was not involved in design or analysis.

  Sources: [Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04752-5); [UWA](https://www.uwa.edu.au/news/article/2026/november/resistance-training-key-to-maintaining-muscle-function-during-glp1-weight-loss); [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261112/Muscle-benefits-of-lifting-weights-while-taking-tirzepatide.aspx); [Archyde](https://www.archyde.com/trial-indicates-tirzepatide-and-exercise-preserves-lean-mass/). *Moderate (single RCT; figures from press coverage; very close match to the user: age 51, postmenopausal-age woman).*
- **Ongoing trials:** LEAN-PREP (n=232, BMI 27–45, 6 months, resistance exercise ± protein during semaglutide/tirzepatide; NCT06885736) — [PubMed 42020128](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42020128/).
- **Nutrition/lifestyle pillars on GLP-1 (GSSI):** nutrient-dense, high-protein diet, adequate hydration and structured progressive resistance exercise to preserve fat-free mass — [GSSI](https://www.gssiweb.org/en/sports-science-exchange/Article/nutritional-and-physical-activity-optimization-for-glp-1-based-therapies).
  - Protein: one cited 2025 expert panel suggests 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day (up to 2.0 with resistance training); a clinic suggests 1.6–2.2 g/kg. Sources conflict, and these recommendations are largely expert opinion rather than RCT-based — [Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20250418/nutrition-exercise-guidance-with-use-of-obesity-medication-may-help-protect-muscle-mass); [apcz.umk.pl review](https://apcz.umk.pl/QS/article/download/74482/47362/253714).
  - **Note:** in severe obesity, protein targets are usually based on *adjusted or ideal* body weight, not 140 kg **[UNVERIFIED]** — defer to the nutrition researcher.
- Practitioner sources warn that excessive cardio on top of a GLP-1-induced deficit may speed muscle loss — [NFPT](https://nfpt.com/resistance-training-programming-for-clients-on-glp-1s). *Very low.*
- **Bipolar disorder:**
  - Evidence on exercise for bipolar symptoms is limited and mixed (2025 meta-analysis: effects unclear, methodological limits) — [PMC12497790](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12497790/).
  - A 2016 review found exercise associated with better depressive symptoms, functioning and quality of life — [PMC4744495](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4744495).
  - Cochrane qualitative synthesis: enjoyable activity in a regular routine at a suitable intensity supports mood management, and routine and rhythm help stability — [Cochrane CD013557](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD013557_factors-influence-physical-activity-bipolar-disorder).
  - In some people, too much exercise may intensify mania — [Harvard Health](https://health.harvard.edu/healthbeat/how-to-create-a-healthy-routine-with-bipolar-disorder). *Low.*
- **Chafing/skin:** no retrieved source addressed this.

### Inferences
- **GLP-1 practicalities:**
  - Schedule exercise at times when nausea is lowest (often not right after an injection or a large meal).
  - Have a small protein-containing snack 1–2 h before resistance sessions if intake is very low.
  - Sip fluids during and after exercise.
  - Reduce training load for 2–3 days after dose escalations if GI side effects are strong.
  - Stop and rest if light-headed; this may be low intake or dehydration rather than hypoglycaemia.
- **Heat:** exercise in cooler parts of the day, use a fan for indoor cycling, and wear breathable clothing. Large bodies dissipate heat less efficiently **[UNVERIFIED]**. This is extra important if she takes lithium or certain antipsychotics **[UNVERIFIED; must be checked with prescriber]**.
- **Chafing/skin [UNVERIFIED, standard advice]:**
  - Anti-chafe balm or stick on inner thighs, under the breasts and in skin folds.
  - Moisture-wicking clothing; long, close-fitting shorts or "thigh bands".
  - A well-fitted high-support sports bra.
  - Shower and dry skin folds thoroughly after exercise.
  - Seek treatment for intertrigo or fungal rash early rather than stopping exercise.
- **Bipolar-specific plan features:**
  - Fixed training times to support circadian rhythm, and avoid vigorous exercise late in the evening.
  - Use a "ceiling" as well as a "floor": don't exceed the planned volume, and a sudden urge to greatly increase training can be an early warning sign to share with her care team.
  - On depressive days, the minimum dose still counts.

### Gaps
- Exact T-REx exercise prescription and full effect sizes (paper not fetched).
- No authoritative source retrieved on: tirzepatide hypoglycaemia risk during exercise (label states low risk unless combined with insulin/sulfonylurea **[UNVERIFIED]**); lithium/heat/dehydration interactions; chafing or intertrigo management in exercisers with obesity; heat tolerance in severe obesity.
- No formal 2025 consensus statement on exercise during GLP-1 therapy was retrieved. The commonly cited 2025 joint advisory (ACLM/ASN/OMA/TOS on nutrition priorities for GLP-1 users) was referenced only second-hand via Healio.

## 8. Program design implications (proposed 12-month phase structure)

### Takeaway
A gated, four-phase program:
1. **Run-in (weeks 1–3):** habit and tolerance.
2. **Foundation (weeks 4–12):** 2–3 resistance sessions/week plus ~60→120 min/week aerobic.
3. **Build (months 4–6):** resistance 3×/week, 2–3 sets; aerobic 150+ min/week.
4. **Strength & capacity / run-prep (months 7–12):** progressive loading, walking 30+ min continuous, strength gates, then run-walk once gates are met.

Progression is triggered by criteria, not the calendar. Resistance training is prioritised throughout because of the T-REx evidence on tirzepatide-associated lean loss. All parameters below are **inferences** from the cited findings (guideline targets, Murphy 2019, Lopes 2019, T-REx, return-to-run criteria). They are not a tested protocol.

### Cited Findings
- Targets: ≥150 min/wk moderate aerobic; resistance ≥2 days/wk; reduce sedentary time; benefits occur without weight loss — [Obesity Canada](https://obesitycanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Physical-Activity-in-Obesity-Management.pdf); [WHO 2020](https://pure.qub.ac.uk/en/publications/world-health-organization-2020-guidelines-on-physical-activity-an/).
- More than 250 min/wk is associated with greater weight loss and less regain — [ACSM 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19127177/).
- Short bouts are equivalent to continuous bouts — [Murphy 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6745307).
- Bands give comparable strength gains to free weights — [Lopes 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6383082).
- Resistance training 3×/wk on tirzepatide reduced lean loss ~25% and increased strength — [UWA/T-REx](https://www.uwa.edu.au/news/article/2026/november/resistance-training-key-to-maintaining-muscle-function-during-glp1-weight-loss).
- Running-readiness criteria — [Physiotutors](https://study.physiotutors.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Return-To-Running-Guidance.pdf); [Sanford](https://WWW.SANFORDHEALTH.ORG/-/media/org/files/medical-professionals/resources-and-education/return-to-running.pdf).

### Inferences: proposed structure

**Before/at start:** mention the plan to the Mounjaro prescriber (see section 1). Record baselines:
- 30-s chair sit-to-stand count
- How many minutes she can walk continuously at a comfortable pace
- Minutes on the bike at RPE 3
- Waist circumference
- Optionally, a 2-min step-in-place count

Repeat these every 4–6 weeks. Strength and stamina gains are motivating even when the scale stalls.

**Phase 0: Run-in (weeks 1–3). Goal: habit, tolerance, technique, no soreness**
- *Aerobic:* "movement snacks" of 3–5 min (indoor walking or easy bike) 2–4×/day at RPE 2–3/10 (CR10; ~10–11 on 6–20; can sing or chat easily). Total ~10–20 min/day, 5–7 days/wk. Week-to-week: add 1 min per bout or one extra bout.
- *Resistance:* 2 non-consecutive days/wk, 5–6 exercises, **1 set × 8–12 reps** at RPE 3–4. Seated or supported versions:
  - sit-to-stand (high seat, hands allowed)
  - wall push-up
  - seated band row
  - seated adduction squeeze (5 × 5-s holds)
  - seated band abduction
  - seated band biceps curl or seated dumbbell triceps extension (1–2 kg)
- *Daily:* break up sitting every 30–60 min (stand and walk 1–2 min).
- *Progression trigger to Phase 1:* completing ≥80% of planned sessions for 2 weeks, no joint pain lasting >24 h, and soreness gone within 48 h.

**Phase 1: Foundation (weeks 4–12). Goal: 2–3× resistance training, ~60→120+ min/wk aerobic**
- *Aerobic:*
  - Bouts grow to 5–10 min, 2–3×/day.
  - Bike 10–15 min continuous by week 12.
  - Intensity RPE 3–4 (moderate; can talk, not sing).
  - One short outdoor walk most days if feasible.
  - From ~week 8, add easy intervals on the bike: 1 min RPE 5 / 2 min RPE 3 × 4–5.
  - Weekly time: add ~10–15 min/week (≈10–20%).
- *Resistance:*
  - 2 days/wk (weeks 4–6) → 3 days/wk (weeks 7–12; Mon/Wed/Fri).
  - 7–8 exercises covering legs/glutes, inner thigh, back, chest/triceps, shoulders/biceps, core and calves.
  - **2 sets × 10–15 reps** at RPE 5–6 (2–4 reps in reserve); 60–90 s rest.
  - **Double progression:** when 2 × 15 reps are achieved at ≤RPE 6 for two consecutive sessions, move to a harder band, +0.5–1 kg, or the next progression level.
- *Skills:* practise floor transfers with chair support 1–2×/week from week 6–8. Introduce bed-based glute bridges and dead bugs.
- *Progression trigger to Phase 2:*
  - ≥15 min continuous bike or walking at RPE 3–4
  - 2 × 15 sit-to-stands from a standard chair without hands (or a clear improvement on baseline)
  - Training ≥2×/wk consistently for 4+ weeks

**Phase 2: Build (months 4–6). Goal: ≥150 min/wk aerobic, 3× resistance, 2–3 sets**
- *Aerobic:*
  - 20–30 min sessions or accumulated bouts, 5–6 days/wk.
  - RPE 3–5 (moderate, with brisk segments).
  - Walking outdoors 20–30 min.
  - 1–2 interval sessions/week (bike or brisk walking): e.g. 6–8 × (1 min RPE 6 / 2 min RPE 3).
  - Target 150 → ~200 min/wk.
- *Resistance:*
  - 3 days/wk; **2–3 sets × 8–12 reps** at RPE 6–7.
  - Standing and floor variants where appropriate: standing band row, incline counter push-up, split-stance or band sit-to-stand, glute bridge with band, side-lying adduction, standing Pallof press, double-leg calf raises with slow lowering.
  - Dumbbells progress toward 3–5 kg for arms.
  - Consider buying heavier bands or loop bands as needed (Lopes 2020 suggests progressive loading matters for body composition).
- *Deload:* every 5th–6th week, halve the sets.
- *Progression trigger to Phase 3:*
  - 30 min continuous walking at moderate pace, pain-free
  - Floor transfers manageable
  - Sit-to-stand with a 5 kg dumbbell × 12
  - Wall/counter push-up × 15

**Phase 3: Strength, capacity and run-prep (months 7–12). Goal: 200–300 min/wk activity; strength gates; possible run-walk start**
- *Aerobic:*
  - Walking 30–45 min, 4–5×/wk, including hills or stairs (stair climbing is a great progress marker).
  - Bike intervals 1–2×/wk at RPE 6–7 in short bouts.
  - Total ~200–300 min/wk as tolerated (ACSM 2009: >250 min/wk aids weight maintenance).
- *Resistance:*
  - 3 days/wk (or a 4-day upper/lower split if she wants more), **3 sets × 6–12 reps** at RPE 7–8.
  - Unilateral work: split squat holding a chair, step-ups to a low step, single-leg glute bridge, one-arm dumbbell row, single-leg calf raise.
  - Core: incline planks, bird-dog.
- *Running readiness gates (all required; physio-derived, not obesity-validated):*
  1. 30 min brisk walking, pain-free, on consecutive days with no next-day swelling.
  2. 15 single-leg sit-to-stands per side (standard chair).
  3. 20 (→25) single-leg calf raises per side.
  4. 30–60 s controlled single-leg balance/squat per side with good knee alignment.
  5. 10–20 pain-free two-foot pogo hops holding a counter.
  6. No current joint pain.
- *Once gates are met:* run-walk 3×/wk on non-consecutive days, e.g. 6–8 × (30 s very easy jog / 2–3 min walk). Progress run segments by ~15–30 s per week only if the previous week was pain-free (adapted Couch-to-5K, repeating weeks freely). Use smooth surfaces, supportive shoes and music.
- *Expectation:* gates may be met anywhere from months 9–15+. Continuous 20–30-min jogging is a realistic year-2 goal.

**Throughout:**
- RPE + talk test as primary intensity tools.
- Minimum-dose "bad day" plan (5-min walk + 3 chair exercises).
- Fixed training times (bipolar stability) with a weekly volume ceiling.
- Hydration, protein, and planning around injections/nausea.
- Chafing prevention.
- Stop rules (chest pain, fainting, unusual breathlessness, dizziness, palpitations → stop; seek urgent care for chest pain or fainting).
- Pain rule: joint pain ≤3/10 that settles within 24 h is acceptable; otherwise regress.

### Gaps
- This phase structure is synthesised from general guidelines and non-obesity-specific protocols. No trial has tested this exact progression in a woman with BMI ~55 on tirzepatide.
- Exact ACSM GETP 12e obesity FITT values, NICE NG246 and EASO wording were not verified (fetch blocked).
- Any physiotherapist or exercise physiologist review (e.g. a Swedish FaR "fysisk aktivitet på recept" prescription via primary care) would add individual safety checks. FaR as a pathway was not researched here.
