# Lean-mass preservation, protein and nutrition practicalities during tirzepatide (Mounjaro) treatment

User profile these notes are calibrated for: woman, 51 y, ~160 cm, ~140 kg (BMI ~55), nearly inactive, starting weekly tirzepatide (schedule unknown), home resistance training (bands, light dumbbells, chair) + low-impact cardio. App = simple protein + water tracker + food ideas, no calorie counting, Swedish context.

**Method note / limitation:** Research done 2026-11-12. In this session, full-text fetching from PubMed Central, NCBI and obesity.org was blocked by the network sandbox, so the findings below come from search-engine extracts of the primary papers (AJCN joint advisory, Obesity Pillars Delphi consensus, DOM SURMOUNT-1 DXA paper, FDA/EMA labels) plus secondary summaries. Where only a secondary source supports a number, this is flagged. To verify the exact wording of the joint advisory and the Delphi statement, the hosts pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov / www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov / www.obesity.org would have to be allowed.

Evidence grading used: **[High]** = RCT / primary trial data or regulatory label; **[Moderate]** = RCT subgroup/post-hoc/exploratory analyses, meta-analyses of heterogeneous data; **[Low]** = expert consensus, narrative review, observational data, mechanistic reasoning; **[Very low]** = blogs/commercial sites, clinical lore.

## 1. How much of the weight lost on tirzepatide/semaglutide is lean mass, and why it matters for a 51-year-old woman

### Takeaway
In the SURMOUNT-1 DXA substudy, about 75% of the weight lost on tirzepatide was fat and about 25% was lean mass, the same split as in placebo/diet. Semaglutide (STEP-1) showed about 60/40. Relative body composition gets better (lean share of body weight rises), and MRI suggests muscle quality improves (less fat inside the muscle). Still, the absolute loss of lean mass is large (about 11% of baseline lean mass), and for a menopausal woman at risk of sarcopenic obesity that matters. Adding resistance training and adequate protein is what guidelines recommend. The evidence that this combination protects lean mass specifically on tirzepatide is still thin. Trials are ongoing.

### Cited Findings
- **SURMOUNT-1 DXA substudy [Moderate; RCT substudy, n=160 of 2539]:** 73% female, mean weight 102.5 kg, BMI 38.0. At week 72, tirzepatide vs placebo changed weight by −21.3% vs −5.3%, fat mass by −33.9% vs −8.2% and lean mass by −10.9% vs −2.6% (all p<0.001). About 75% of the weight lost was fat and 25% lean, **for both tirzepatide and placebo**, and this held across most subgroups — [Look et al., Diabetes Obes Metab 2025, DOI 10.1111/dom.16275 (PMC11965027)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11965027/); [CiteDrive record](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/body-composition-changes-during-weight-reduction-with-tirzepatide-in-the-scpsurmountscp1-study-of-adults-with-obesity-or-overweight/)
- The improvement in the fat-to-lean ratio was similar across age groups (about −0.25 for <50 y, −0.24 for 50–<65 y, −0.25 for ≥65 y). This comes from the ObesityWeek 2022 abstract as reported by conference coverage — [MDedge conference coverage](https://www.mdedge.com/endocrinology/article/263121/obesity/staggering-weight-loss-and-benefits-body-composition/page/0/1); [Patient Care Online](https://www.patientcareonline.com/view/tirzepatide-led-to-clinically-meaningful-improvements-in-body-composition-in-adults-with-obesity-overweight) [Moderate/secondary]
- **STEP-1 semaglutide DXA [Moderate; exploratory analysis]:** lean mass was about 39% of total weight lost in STEP-1 (and 40% in SUSTAIN 8). Across GLP-1 RA studies the lean share ranged from near 0% to about 40% — [Why you should not skip tailored exercise interventions… (PMC11300307)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11300307/). Another summary attributed to the joint advisory gives about 8.3 kg fat (62%) vs 5.3 kg lean (38%). One blog says "~45%", which conflicts with the others and should be disregarded — [ResearchGate: STEP 1 body composition](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/353140244_Impact_of_Semaglutide_on_Body_Composition_in_Adults_With_Overweight_or_Obesity_Exploratory_Analysis_of_the_STEP_1_Study)
- STEP-1 lifestyle counselling, which encouraged 150 min/week of activity, did **not** prevent lean-mass loss — [PMC11300307](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11300307/) [Moderate]
- **Muscle quality – SURPASS-3 MRI (tirzepatide, T2D) [Moderate; post-hoc]:** tirzepatide reduced thigh muscle volume but also lowered intramuscular fat infiltration more than population-based estimates would predict, which the authors read as better muscle composition. The lean loss was "comparable to that seen in the general population" for the weight lost — [Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol 2025, SURPASS-3 MRI](https://www.thelancet.com/pdfs/journals/landia/PIIS2213-8587(25)00027-0.pdf); [commentary PMC12950923](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12950923/)
- **Adaptive vs maladaptive debate [Low; review/opinion]:** Neeland and colleagues argue that lean-mass loss on GLP-1 RA is roughly proportional to total weight loss (about 15–45% in the literature) and is largely adaptive, helped by better insulin sensitivity and less muscle fat — [Circulation 2024, "Muscle Mass and GLP-1 RA: Adaptive or Maladaptive?"](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.124.067676)
- DXA "lean mass" includes water, organs and bone, so it is an imperfect proxy for skeletal muscle. MRI/CT are better — [Muscle health in the era of incretin therapies (PMC12825403)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12825403/) [Low]
- **Sarcopenic obesity:** the ESPEN/EASO 2022 consensus defines it as obesity plus sarcopenia (low muscle function plus altered body composition). One application of the criteria uses grip strength <18 kg in women and fat % >41% in women as cut-offs — [ESPEN/EASO consensus](https://snu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/definition-and-diagnostic-criteria-for-sarcopenic-obesity-espen-a-2/); [application study (PMC11222587)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11222587/) [Low; consensus]
- **Menopause:** a 2026 narrative review argues that the menopausal transition raises sarcopenic-obesity risk through inflammation, adipokine changes and impaired insulin signalling, and calls for menopause-specific definitions — [Adipose–muscle crosstalk in midlife women (PMC13180594)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13180594/) [Low]
- A 2026 review concludes that lean mass falls alongside fat mass on GLP-1 RA and GLP-1/GIP agonists. In its view the bigger long-term risk may be weight regain after stopping the drug (fat regained faster than muscle), not direct muscle harm. It favours resistance training plus protein spread across meals — [Frontiers Endocrinol 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/endocrinology/articles/10.3389/fendo.2026.1805067/full); [GLP-1 pharmacotherapy and muscle narrative review (PMC13667052)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13667052) [Low]
- SEMALEAN (semaglutide, real-world): 12 months of semaglutide reduced the prevalence of sarcopenic obesity by 16% — [SEMALEAN (PMC12673431)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12673431/) [Low/Moderate; observational]
- Head-to-head comparisons of lean-mass loss on tirzepatide vs bariatric surgery or diet alone were not found. A trial registry entry notes only that lifestyle and bariatric surgery have "confirmed benefits" — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07457437](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07457437)

### Inferences
- For this user: if she loses about 20% (about 28 kg) and the 25% lean rule holds, roughly 7 kg would be lean mass. That is a meaningful amount for someone who is inactive, post-/peri-menopausal and starting from low muscle strength relative to body weight. In absolute terms, her starting lean mass is probably high because of her body size (people with severe obesity carry more lean tissue). What matters most is **function**: rising from a chair, stairs, grip.
- The fact that placebo showed the same 75/25 split suggests tirzepatide is not uniquely muscle-wasting. It is what happens when you lose weight, and the drug just makes you lose more. This framing helps an app avoid fear messaging ("you'll lose muscle"). The better message is "help your body keep strength while you lose weight".
- For the app, strength and function (e.g., a 30-second chair-stand test, how many stairs) are more meaningful and measurable at home than lean mass.
- Background (not verified in this session): the classic "quarter-FFM rule" for diet-induced weight loss (about 25% of weight lost is fat-free mass) and data from bariatric surgery (often about 20–30% FFM in the first year) put tirzepatide in the same range. Treat this as unverified context, not a cited finding.

### Gaps
- The full SURMOUNT-1 DXA text (sex-specific and BMI ≥40 subgroups) could not be fetched. Whether people with BMI >50 lose a different lean proportion is unknown.
- No muscle-function (strength/physical performance) outcome data from SURMOUNT trials were found.
- No head-to-head data comparing tirzepatide with bariatric surgery or lifestyle for lean mass or muscle quality.

## 2. Do resistance training and higher protein reduce lean-mass loss during GLP-1 RA weight loss?

### Takeaway
The direct RCT evidence on GLP-1 RA plus exercise is limited to liraglutide: the Lundgren 2021 NEJM trial showed that combining it with exercise roughly doubled fat loss and best preserved body composition. For tirzepatide/semaglutide plus resistance training, randomized trials (e.g., HUSTLE) are still ongoing as of 2026. Expert consensus (2025 joint advisory, 2025 Delphi) is firm that protein **must be combined** with resistance training. Protein alone is considered insufficient. Evidence for the combination is extrapolated from caloric-restriction studies [Low–Moderate].

### Cited Findings
- **Lundgren et al., NEJM 2021 (S-LiTE) [High for weight/fat outcomes]:** after an 8-week low-calorie diet (~13 kg lost), 195 adults were randomized for 1 year to exercise + placebo, liraglutide 3.0 mg, liraglutide + exercise, or placebo. Total weight loss from before the diet was 15.7% (combination), 13.4% (liraglutide), 10.9% (exercise) and 6.7% (placebo). The combination beat exercise alone (−5.4 kg, p=0.004) but did not significantly beat liraglutide alone (−2.7 kg, p=0.13). The reductions in fat mass/body-fat % were about twice as large in the combination group as with either single treatment. Partly Novo Nordisk-funded — [Healio](https://cm.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20210506/combination-liraglutide-plus-exercise-improves-weightloss-maintenance-in-obesity); [PACE-CME](https://pace-cme.org/2021/06/15/weight-loss-maintenance-in-obesity-more-effective-with-glp-1ra-and-exercise-than-either-treatment-alone); [Full text PDF mirror](https://gwern.net/doc/longevity/glp/semaglutide/2021-lundgren.pdf)
- Body-fat % fell by about 3.9 percentage points with the combination vs about 1.9 (liraglutide) and 1.7 (exercise). A secondary table lists lean-mass change as 0.0% for liraglutide alone and +0.8% for liraglutide + exercise. These are secondary numbers, not verified against the NEJM tables — [PMC11300307](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11300307/) [Moderate/secondary]
- **Joint advisory (ACLM/ASN/OMA/TOS, Mozaffarian et al., AJCN 2025;122:344–367) [Low; expert advisory]:** "increased protein intake alone is likely inadequate to support the preservation of muscle mass in the absence of structured resistance/strength training". The advisory lists 8 priorities: patient-centred initiation, baseline nutrition assessment, GI side-effect management, nutrient-dense minimally processed diet, preventing micronutrient deficiency, adequate protein **plus** strength training, etc. A corrigendum has been published — [AJCN full text](https://ajcn.nutrition.org/article/S0002-9165(25)00240-0/fulltext); [PMC12264624](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12264624/); [Newswise summary](https://www.newswise.com/articles/nutritional-priorities-to-support-glp-1-therapy-for-obesity); [ACC journal scan](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/journal-scans/2025/05/30/16/10/nutritional-and-lifestyle-strategies-support-glp-1-obesity-treatment)
- **Delphi consensus (Obesity Pillars 2025, DOI 10.1016/j.obpill.2025.100228) [Low]:** 15-member international panel, 52 statements, 7 modules covering nutrition, physical activity and monitoring with GLP-1-based therapies. Agreement was defined as ≥67% rating ≥7 on a 9-point scale. The authors state the evidence is "primarily indirect" and that direct evidence is "urgently needed" — [PMC12768930](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12768930/); [Nestlé Medical Hub summary](https://www.nestlemedicalhub.com/evidence/nutritional-and-lifestyle-supportive-care-recommendations-for-management-of-obesity-with-glp-1-based-therapies)
- RCTs testing high protein plus resistance training on GLP-1 RA/tirzepatide are registered and ongoing (HUSTLE: NCT07700030; NCT07457437; NCT07154719; GLP-1s to Enhance Lasting Optimal Weight NCT06861439) — [HUSTLE](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07700030); [NCT07457437](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07457437)
- A review argues that generic exercise advice is "clinically incomplete" and that training prescriptions should be adapted to the drugs' GI and behavioural side effects. Practical sources suggest 2–3 (some say 3–4) resistance sessions per week — [GLP-1 exercise review (apcz.umk.pl)](https://apcz.umk.pl/QS/article/download/74482/47362/253714) [Low]; [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/exercise-for-glp-1-use) [Low]

### Inferences
- The best evidence-based strategy is resistance training 2–3×/week (whole-body, progressive) plus a protein target, rather than protein alone. For a deconditioned 140 kg woman, starting volume should be very low (e.g., 1 set of 4–6 exercises, sit-to-stand from a chair, wall/counter push-ups, band rows), progressing slowly. Joint and fall safety come first.
- The app's tracking unit for exercise should be "strength sessions this week" (goal 2, later 3) plus daily steps/minutes. Fine-grained metrics are not needed.

### Gaps
- No completed RCT of tirzepatide + structured resistance training with DXA/MRI outcomes was found (as of Nov 2026, in search results).
- Lean-mass outcomes of the Lundgren trial were only available via a secondary table.

## 3. Protein targets: g/kg, reference body weight, per-meal distribution, minimum with low appetite – and a concrete target for this person

### Takeaway
Current GLP-1 nutrition guidance gives **1.2–2.0 g/kg adjusted body weight/day** during active weight loss, or simply **80–120 g/day** (2025 joint advisory). The Delphi consensus floor is **≥0.8 g/kg/day**. There is **no consensus** on which reference weight to use in severe obesity, and the advisory says so explicitly. Per meal, about 25–30 g (about 0.4 g/kg; about 2.5–3 g leucine) is the anabolic threshold for older adults. For this person (adjusted BW about 74–87 kg), a practical target is **about 90–100 g/day**, with a "bad-day floor" of **about 60 g/day**.

### Cited Findings
- **Joint advisory 2025 [Low; expert opinion]:** 1.2–2.0 g/kg **adjusted** body weight/day during major weight reduction. As a simpler alternative that may aid adherence: **80–120 g/day** (about 16–24% of energy on 2000 kcal/day). The advisory notes the RDA of 0.8 g/kg/day is under review by the National Academies — [PMC12264624](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12264624/); [SOCHOB PDF mirror](https://www.sochob.cl/web1/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Nutritional-priorities-to-support-GLP-1-therapy-for-obesity.pdf)
- The joint advisory states there is **no consensus** on whether to base protein targets on actual, adjusted/ideal body weight or fat-free mass — [epocrates summary](https://www.epocrates.com/online/article/when-weight-loss-drugs-work-dont-let-nutrition-and-muscle-fall-behind) (secondary; consistent with advisory text)
- Other formulations: 1.0–1.5 g/kg adjusted BW/day with **at least 60 g/day** (epocrates clinical summary) — [epocrates](https://www.epocrates.com/online/article/when-weight-loss-drugs-work-dont-let-nutrition-and-muscle-fall-behind) [Low]. 1.2–1.5 g/kg **actual** BW/day, or 25–30% of energy on a 1600 kcal diet (Nestlé Health Science consensus-based document) — [Nestlé Medical Hub 2026](https://www.nestlemedicalhub.com/sites/default/files/2026-02/Considerations%20for%20the%20Wight%20Loss%20Phase%20_2_3_2026.pdf) [Low; industry]. Note that using actual BW at BMI 55 gives an implausible 168–210 g/day, and sources caution that actual-BW calculations "may not be appropriate" in severe obesity or kidney disease.
- **Delphi consensus 2025:** protein ≥0.8 g/kg/day as the floor — [PMC12768930](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12768930/) [Low]
- **Per-meal:** PROT-AGE/older-adult literature puts the anabolic threshold at about 25–30 g of protein per meal (about 2.5–2.8 g leucine), or about 0.4 g/kg per meal. ESPEN: 1.0–1.2 g/kg/day for healthy older adults, 1.2–1.5 g/kg/day for those with acute/chronic illness. PROT-AGE: ≥1.0–1.2 g/kg/day, and ≥1.2 if exercising — [Frontiers Nutr 2024 mini-review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2024.1394916/full); [Paddon-Jones sarcopenia handout](https://www.nestlemedicalhub.com/sites/site.prod.nestlemedicalhub.com/files/2021-03/HandoutAddressingSarcopeniaPaddonJones6pp.pdf); [PMC11150820](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11150820/) [Moderate for MPS physiology; Low for clinical outcomes]
- DACH nutrition societies raised the older-adult reference to 1.0 g/kg/day, so national references vary — [Frontiers Nutr 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2024.1394916/full)
- Adjusted body weight is commonly defined as IBW + 0.25 × (actual − IBW). One source uses target weight + 25% of the difference. Verify with a clinical reference — [glpkeeper blog](https://glpkeeper-web-staging.pages.dev/blog/protein-guide-glp1) [Very low]

### Inferences (calculation for this user)
- Height 160 cm (≈63.0 in), weight 140 kg.
  - IBW (Devine, female: 45.5 + 2.3 × inches over 60) ≈ **52.4 kg**. Reference weight at BMI 25 = 25 × 1.6² = **64 kg**.
  - Adjusted BW (0.25 factor) = 52.4 + 0.25 × (140 − 52.4) ≈ **74 kg**. With the 0.4 factor used in some nutrition-support settings ≈ **87 kg**. With the BMI-25 reference weight as the base ≈ **83 kg**.
  - 1.2 g/kg × 74–87 kg ≈ **89–104 g/day**. 1.5 g/kg × 74 ≈ 111 g. The 2.0 g/kg upper bound (148–174 g) is unrealistic with suppressed appetite.
  - The joint advisory's absolute 80–120 g/day overlaps.
  - **Recommended app target: 90 g/day (range 80–110 g) as the default**, presented as "about 90 g". Optional "stretch" of 100–110 g once on stable doses and training regularly.
  - **Minimum ("bad-day floor"): about 60 g/day.** This is ≈0.8 g/kg × 74 kg = 59 g, matching the Delphi floor and the epocrates "≥60 g/day". On high-nausea days, protein drinks/dairy carry this.
  - **Per meal: 25–30 g × 3 meals ≈ 75–90 g, plus one 10–20 g snack/drink.** If she can only manage small meals: 4–5 "mini-meals" of 15–20 g each.
- Kidney caveat: high protein targets assume normal kidney function. The app should say "check with your care team if you have kidney disease".
- Priority order when appetite is very low: (1) fluids, (2) protein first on the plate, (3) vegetables/fibre, (4) other carbohydrates and fat. This "protein first" ordering is consensus practice, not RCT-tested.

### Gaps
- Exact wording of the joint advisory's protein section (adjusted BW definition, per-meal advice) could not be verified in full text because fetches were blocked.
- No GLP-1-specific RCT compares protein targets (e.g., 1.0 vs 1.5 g/kg) for lean-mass outcomes.

## 4. Hydration, fibre and constipation

### Takeaway
GLP-1/GIP drugs reduce thirst and food intake, which also removes food water. GI losses (vomiting, diarrhoea) can cause dehydration, and the label warns this can lead to acute kidney injury, sometimes requiring dialysis. Consensus says **>2 L of fluid/day**, more with exercise, vomiting, diarrhoea or heat, and fibre **≥25 g/day for women** (psyllium if diet falls short), titrated to tolerance. Constipation is common, especially at lower doses.

### Cited Findings
- **Label warning [High; regulatory]:** GI adverse reactions may lead to dehydration, which if severe can cause acute kidney injury. Postmarketing reports include AKI requiring haemodialysis, mostly in patients with nausea, vomiting or diarrhoea leading to dehydration. Monitor renal function in patients with reactions that could lead to volume depletion, and advise patients about dehydration risk — [FDA Mounjaro label 2025](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/215866s039lbl.pdf); [DailyMed](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=d2d7da5d-ad07-4228-955f-cf7e355c8cc0); [Health Canada monograph](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00073189.PDF)
- **Delphi consensus [Low]:** fluids **>2 L/day**, with extra attention during exercise, diarrhoea, vomiting and fasting. Fibre ≥25 g/day (women), ≥30 g/day (men). Adequate fibre with supplementation (e.g., psyllium) if diet falls short, adjusted to individual tolerance — [PMC12768930](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12768930/); [Nestlé Medical Hub summary](https://www.nestlemedicalhub.com/evidence/nutritional-and-lifestyle-supportive-care-recommendations-for-management-of-obesity-with-glp-1-based-therapies)
- **Joint advisory [Low]:** encourage adequate fluids and fibre from foods — [PMC12264624](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12264624/)
- Constipation incidence on tirzepatide (SURMOUNT-1+2 pooled per label table, as reproduced by secondary site): 5% placebo, 17% (5 mg), 14% (10 mg), 11% (15 mg). Constipation is the one GI effect that does **not** rise with dose — [formblends label reproduction](https://formblends.com/articles/glp1-hub/tirzepatide-side-effects-complete-guide-2026) [Moderate/secondary]; primary: [Zepbound FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/217806Orig1s020lbl.pdf)
- Practical sources recommend hydration plus electrolytes because GI effects raise dehydration risk, especially around exercise — [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/exercise-for-glp-1-use) [Low]

### Inferences
- Water target for this user: **2.0 L/day of drinks as the default** (≈8 glasses of 2.5 dl), shown as glasses rather than mL. Add **+0.5 L on training days or warm days**, and **+0.5–1 L plus an oral rehydration solution (vätskeersättning, available at Swedish pharmacies) on vomiting/diarrhoea days**. Larger body size and higher sweat losses make 2.0 L a floor, not a ceiling. Background (not verified here): EFSA's adequate total water intake for women is 2.0 L/day from all sources, about 1.6 L of it from drinks. Her reduced food intake means less water from food, which supports ≥2 L of drinks.
- Override caveat: people with heart failure or advanced kidney disease may have fluid restrictions. The app should defer to care-team instructions.
- Fibre should be increased gradually, together with fluid. Fibre without fluid can worsen constipation (physiologically plausible; consistent with the Delphi "titrate to tolerance").

### Gaps
- No GLP-1-specific trial of fluid targets was found. The >2 L figure is consensus.
- No source compared fibre types (soluble/psyllium vs insoluble) specifically under GLP-1 RA.

## 5. GI side effects: frequency, timing (dose escalation, injection day), management and red flags

### Takeaway
Nausea (about 25–33%), diarrhoea (about 19–23%), vomiting (about 8–13%) and constipation (about 11–17%) are common on tirzepatide. They are mostly mild-to-moderate and **concentrated during dose escalation**, then decrease over time. Dosing (EU SmPC): 2.5 mg/week × 4 weeks → 5 mg, then +2.5 mg steps at ≥4-week intervals up to 15 mg max. Management: small meals, stop at first fullness, avoid fatty/fried food, eat slowly, fluids between meals. Red flags: severe persistent abdominal pain (pancreatitis), right-upper-quadrant pain/fever/jaundice (gallbladder), inability to keep fluids down or signs of dehydration (AKI risk).

### Cited Findings
- **Dosing schedule (EU/Sweden) [High; regulatory]:** start 2.5 mg once weekly. After 4 weeks increase to 5 mg. Further increases in 2.5 mg steps after at least 4 weeks on the current dose. Max 15 mg weekly. Maintenance doses 5/10/15 mg. 2.5 mg is a starting (titration) dose — [EMA Mounjaro product information (sv)](https://www.ema.europa.eu/sv/documents/product-information/mounjaro-epar-product-information_sv.pdf); [FASS bipacksedel](https://fass.se/product/20231004000022/pl); [Lilly Sverige](https://www.lilly.com/se/hcp/obesitas/lakemedel)
- **Incidence – obesity trials [High]:** label table (SURMOUNT-1+2 pooled, up to 72 weeks) as reproduced: nausea 8% placebo vs 25%/29%/28% (5/10/15 mg). Diarrhoea 8% vs 19%/21%/23%. Vomiting 2% vs 8%/11%/13%. Constipation 5% vs 17%/14%/11%. SURMOUNT-1-only figures cited elsewhere: nausea 24.6–33.3% vs 9.5% placebo, diarrhoea 18.7–23.0% vs 7.3%. The secondary sources label the trial pools inconsistently — [formblends](https://formblends.com/articles/glp1-hub/tirzepatide-side-effects-complete-guide-2026); [telehealthally](https://telehealthally.com/guides/tirzepatide-side-effects-guide); primary [Zepbound FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/217806Orig1s020lbl.pdf)
- **Timing [High]:** "the majority of nausea, vomiting, and/or diarrhea events occurred during dose escalation and decreased over time" — [Zepbound FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/217806Orig1s020lbl.pdf). The label instructs following the escalation schedule to reduce GI adverse reactions — [FDA Mounjaro label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/215866s039lbl.pdf)
- Mounjaro (T2D) placebo-controlled pool: any GI adverse reaction 20.4% placebo vs 37.1%/39.6%/43.6% (5/10/15 mg). Severe GI reactions 0.9% placebo vs 1.3%/0.4%/1.2% — [Lilly HCP: How Mounjaro works](https://mounjaro.lilly.com/hcp/how-mounjaro-works); [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/215866s039lbl.pdf)
- Not recommended in severe gastroparesis — [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/215866s039lbl.pdf)
- **Gallbladder [Moderate]:** meta-analysis: tirzepatide is associated with gallbladder/biliary disease (RR 1.52, 95% CI 1.17–1.98) and cholelithiasis (RR 1.67, 1.14–2.44), with no clear dose-response — [J Diabetes Investig 2024 meta-analysis (PubMed 39569606)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39569606/). In SURMOUNT-1, gallbladder events were more frequent at 10/15 mg than placebo (mainly cholelithiasis). Secondary sources give about 1.5–1.9% vs 0.5–0.6% — [Frontiers Endocrinol 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/endocrinology/articles/10.3389/fendo.2023.1214334/text)
- **Pancreatitis [Moderate]:** SURMOUNT-1 acute pancreatitis about 0.2% tirzepatide vs 0.1% placebo (secondary). A 2023 meta-analysis concluded tirzepatide appears safe regarding pancreatitis risk, but case reports exist — [Frontiers Endocrinol 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/endocrinology/articles/10.3389/fendo.2023.1214334/text); [case report PMC11743417](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11743417/)
- **Management (joint advisory) [Low]:** GI side-effect management is one of the 8 priorities. Lower-viscosity foods and adequate fluids are suggested — [PMC12264624](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12264624/)
- GIP agonism may partly mitigate GLP-1-induced nausea centrally (mechanistic review) — [PMC11830610](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11830610/) [Low; mechanistic]

### Inferences
- **Highest-risk windows for this user:** (a) the first 1–2 weeks of each new dose step, i.e., weeks 1, 5, 9, 13… if titrated every 4 weeks, and (b) within a few days after each weekly injection. Point (b) is widely reported clinically but not quantified in the sources found [Very low]. The app should let the user log her injection day so it can show "likely tougher days" adaptively from her own logs, not from fixed assumptions.
- Standard practical advice (consistent across clinical sources; RCT-level evidence for individual tips is lacking): eat small portions; eat slowly and stop at the first sign of fullness; avoid large, fatty, fried or very sweet meals; separate drinking from eating if fullness is a problem (sip between meals); bland, cold or room-temperature foods on nausea days; do not lie down right after eating; don't force food when full, but keep protein + fluids.
- **Red flags (seek care, 1177 / emergency):** severe persistent abdominal pain, possibly radiating to the back, with or without vomiting (pancreatitis). Pain under the right ribs, fever, yellow skin/eyes (gallbladder). Unable to keep fluids down for about 24 h, very little or dark urine, dizziness/fainting (dehydration → AKI risk). Signs of allergic reaction. Low blood sugar symptoms if she is also on insulin/sulfonylurea. These come from label warnings. The 24 h threshold is a reasonable app heuristic, not a label number.

### Gaps
- No quantitative source on the within-week timing of nausea (days after injection) was found. This is anecdotal/clinical lore.
- The EU SmPC section 4.8 frequency table could not be fetched directly. The figures above come from the FDA label via secondary reproduction.

## 6. Exercise timing relative to injection day and side-effect days

### Takeaway
There is **no trial evidence** on timing exercise relative to the weekly injection. Practical (mostly commercial/clinical-education) guidance is to keep injection day and the 1–2 days after light, put strength sessions on the days when side effects have eased, avoid intense training during active nausea, and hydrate/take electrolytes.

### Cited Findings
- Some sources propose light activity on injection day, cardio or rest the next 1–2 days, and the hardest resistance sessions on about days 3–5 after injection. Others say many people schedule workouts 3–4 days after injection. A patient-education source says there are "no strict rules" — [doctronic blog](https://www.doctronic.ai/blog/glp-1-exercise-guide/); [peak-protocol](https://peak-protocol.pages.dev/glp-1/glp1-exercise-guide/); [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/exercise-for-glp-1-use) [Very low/Low]
- Avoid intense training during active nausea. Watch for lightheadedness/fatigue, start with walking and low-load resistance, and get medical advice for severe nausea, dizziness or chest pain — [WTOP news](https://wtop.com/news/2025/02/exercising-on-glp-1s-how-to-stay-safe-and-healthy); [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/exercise-for-glp-1-use) [Low]
- A review recommends adapting exercise prescription to GLP-1 GI/behavioural side effects — [apcz.umk.pl review](https://apcz.umk.pl/QS/article/download/74482/47362/253714) [Low]

### Inferences
- Flexible rule for the app: plan 2–3 strength sessions per week on "good days". If she logs moderate/severe nausea, swap to a 10-minute gentle walk or mobility session ("mini-pass") rather than skipping entirely. Never prompt hard sessions on a logged vomiting/diarrhoea day. Prompt a small protein snack + water about 1 h before training if appetite allows (low evidence; prevents lightheadedness).

### Gaps
- No RCT or cohort data on injection-cycle timing of exercise.

## 7. Practical protein-dense foods for low appetite (Swedish context) and micronutrients/supplements

### Takeaway
Dairy (kvarg, skyr, keso, milk/fil) and eggs are the most practical, cheap, soft, high-leucine Swedish staples for low appetite. Liquids and semi-solids (shakes, drinkable yoghurt, soups) are best tolerated on bad days. Micronutrient shortfalls are documented with GLP-1 RA, especially vitamin D, iron, calcium and potassium/magnesium. B12 is a concern mainly with metformin co-use. A daily multivitamin/mineral plus vitamin D is a commonly recommended "safety net" based on expert opinion and observational data. An RCT is ongoing.

### Cited Findings
- Swedish kvarg products: Arla Mild Kvarg about 9.7 g protein/100 g; Valio Profeel proteinkvarg about 9.8 g/100 g; Arla färskost med kvarg about 11 g/100 g. Kvarg has about 3× the protein of milk (Lindahls/Skånemejerier). Livsmedelsverket's database has analysed kvarg — [Open Food Facts: Arla Mild kvarg](https://se.openfoodfacts.org/product/4016241043047/mild-kvarg-vanilj-arla); [Valio Profeel](https://www.profeel.com/se/produkter/valio-profeel-proteinkvarg-vanilj.md); [Lindahls Proteinrapport 2025](https://foretag.skanemejerier.se/content/uploads/2025/08/Proteinrapporten_Lindahls.pdf); [Livsmedelsverket project info](https://soknaringsinnehall.livsmedelsverket.se/Home/ProjectInfo/184)
- **Micronutrients [Low; observational/review]:** a 2026 narrative review found vitamin D deficiency was the most common abnormality on GLP-1 RA (7.5% at 6 months, 13.6% at 12 months). Ferritin was 26–30% lower than in SGLT2i comparators, and >60% of users were below estimated requirements for calcium and iron — [PubMed 41549912](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41549912/). A May 2025 intake study found the largest shortfalls in vitamin D, potassium, choline, magnesium and iron, while B12 intake was adequate — [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20250514/glp-1-weight-loss-drugs-leave-users-low-on-key-nutrients-study-finds.aspx); [Harvard Health](https://content.health.harvard.edu/blog/study-taking-glp-1-drugs-may-increase-risk-of-key-nutrient-deficiencies)
- Expert/pharmacy guidance: consider vitamin D and B12 or a multivitamin. A daily multivitamin is a reasonable safety net, especially in the first months — [Pharmacy Times](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/pharmacists-are-crucial-partners-in-guiding-nutrition-and-supplement-use-with-glp-1s); [SingleCare](https://www.singlecare.com/blog/how-pharmacists-can-prevent-nutrient-deficiencies-from-glp1s/) [Low]
- Joint advisory: baseline nutritional assessment, preventing micronutrient deficiencies, and micronutrient or protein supplementation "as needed" are among its priorities — [PMC12264624](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12264624/) [Low]
- An RCT of multivitamin vs placebo (12 months; outcomes B12, vitamin D, ferritin) in GLP-1 users is registered. No results yet — [Epistemonikos record](https://www.epistemonikos.org/en/documents/c6cc80f5858b088e5014fb483af033b2222ee0dd)
- Nutrition-gap lessons from bariatric surgery are being applied to GLP-1 RA weight loss — [PMC12913018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12913018/) [Low]

### Inferences
- For this user: a standard multivitamin-mineral (with iron if still menstruating or iron-low) plus vitamin D. Sweden's latitude makes D-vitamin relevant. Background (not verified here): Livsmedelsverket's general advice is 10 µg/day for most adults, 20 µg for ≥75 y, and higher doses only via healthcare. B12 matters if she also takes metformin or eats little meat/dairy. Supplements should be discussed with her care team, which can check baseline labs (vitamin D, ferritin/Hb, B12, kidney function, electrolytes).
- **Malnutrition risk:** with very low intake for weeks (e.g., only a few hundred kcal or <50 g protein/day), the risk of muscle loss, micronutrient deficiency, fatigue, hair loss and gallstones rises. The app should flag sustained very low intake ("you've logged under 50 g protein 5 of the last 7 days — talk to your nurse/dietitian").

### Gaps
- Livsmedelsverket's own per-100 g values for keso, skyr, kvarg naturell, etc. could not be retrieved. Values below are approximate typical label values.

## App design implications (concrete numbers for this user)

**1. Daily protein target: 90 g/day (shown as "about 90 g"), acceptable range 80–110 g. Bad-day floor 60 g.**
- Reasoning: the joint advisory (2025) gives 1.2–2.0 g/kg *adjusted* BW or simply 80–120 g/day. For 160 cm/140 kg, adjusted BW ≈ 74 kg (IBW 52.4 kg + 25% of excess) to about 87 kg (40% factor). 1.2 g/kg × 74–87 ≈ 89–104 g. Actual-weight math (168+ g) is inappropriate at BMI 55 and unachievable with suppressed appetite. The floor of 60 g ≈ 0.8 g/kg adjusted BW (Delphi floor; "≥60 g/day" in clinical summaries).
- Settings: allow the care team/user to override (kidney disease → clinician-set target). Re-compute the adjusted BW every 10 kg lost (e.g., at 120 kg: AdjBW ≈ 69 kg → target stays ≈ 85–90 g. The target barely changes, so a fixed 90 g is fine for the first year).
- Display: progress ring with three zones: <60 g "tough day – that's OK, try a drink", 60–80 g "good", ≥80–90 g "goal reached". Never shame. Celebrate streaks of "≥60 g days".

**2. Per-meal target: 25–30 g protein × 3 meals + 1 protein snack/drink of 10–20 g.**
- Low-appetite mode (toggle, or auto-suggested after a logged nausea day): 5 mini-meals × 15–20 g. "Protein first" prompt: eat the protein part of the plate first.
- Simplest logging: tap food "portions" (icons) that each carry a pre-set protein value. No grams of food, no calories.

**3. Water target: 2.0 L/day (8 glasses à 2.5 dl) default.**
- +0.5 L (2 glasses) on strength/cardio days and hot days.
- +0.5–1 L plus a nudge to use vätskeersättning (oral rehydration) when vomiting/diarrhoea is logged.
- Tips: sip between meals rather than large volumes with meals (fullness). Warm/cold drinks, broth (buljong) and milk count. Milk and drinkable yoghurt count for both water and protein.
- Override for clinician-set fluid restriction.

**4. Fibre/constipation:** daily optional check "Did you have a bowel movement today? yes/no". After 3 days with "no" → tips (fluids, kiwi/prunes, oat/psyllium (loppfrö/fiberhusk) introduced slowly with water, walk) and "talk to your nurse if no improvement / pain". Fibre goal ≥25 g/day stays in the background (not tracked numerically): prompt "1 fibre item per meal" (vegetables, berries, oats, rye crispbread (knäckebröd), lentils/beans).

**5. Side-effect logging design**
- Fields: injection date + dose (2.5 / 5 / 7.5 / 10 / 12.5 / 15 mg) and site. Daily 0–3 scale (none/mild/moderate/severe) for nausea, vomiting (count), diarrhoea (count), constipation, heartburn/reflux, abdominal pain, fatigue, dizziness. Appetite 0–3. Optional free text.
- Auto-context: show "day X after injection" and "week Y on this dose". Highlight first 2 weeks after a dose increase as the expected peak window (label: most GI events occur during escalation and decrease over time).
- Personal pattern view after 3–4 injections: "Your nausea usually peaks on day 2 after injection" (learned from her logs, not assumed).
- Adaptive coaching: moderate/severe nausea → switch to low-appetite food list + liquid protein suggestions + gentle walk instead of strength session. Vomiting/diarrhoea → hydration mode (+water target, ORS).
- **Red-flag screen (hard stop, link to 1177 / call 112):** severe abdominal pain that doesn't go away (especially radiating to the back); pain under right ribs + fever or yellow eyes/skin; can't keep fluids down ~24 h, very little/dark urine, fainting; vomiting blood/black stools; allergic reaction signs. Also a softer flag: "≥3 days in a row moderate–severe vomiting/diarrhoea, or protein <50 g on 5 of 7 days → contact your clinic".
- Export summary (PDF/share) for clinic visits: dose history, side-effect trends, average protein/water, strength sessions per week.

**6. Exercise scheduling tie-in:** goal 2 strength sessions/week at the start (later 3), placed by default on the days her own logs show as "good" (often ≥2–3 days after injection, but personalised). A 10-minute "mini-pass" fallback on bad days. Daily low-impact movement minutes (walking, sit-to-stand, cycling) separately. Functional check every 4 weeks: 30-second chair-stand count, which is easy at home and tracks muscle function better than the scale.

**9. Tone/positioning:** the evidence supports "keep strength and fuel your body" messaging, not muscle-loss fear. About 25% of tirzepatide weight loss is lean mass, the same proportion as with diet alone, and muscle fat content improves. Resistance training plus about 90 g protein is the consensus-backed (low-to-moderate evidence) way to maximise the fat share of loss and preserve function.
